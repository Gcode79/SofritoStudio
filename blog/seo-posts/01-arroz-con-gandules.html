Arroz con Gandules — Authentic Puerto Rican Rice Recipe

The national dish of Puerto Rico — fragrant rice with pigeon peas, sofrito, sazón, and the crispiest pegao you've ever tasted.

⏱ 60 min 🍽 8 servings ⭐ 4.9

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·12 min read

Jump to Recipe

There are exactly two types of Puerto Rican families: those who make arroz con gandules every Sunday, and those who are lying about it. In the Ortiz household, this wasn't just dinner — it was the ritual that held the week together. My abuela would start the sofrito sizzling before the rest of us were even awake, and by noon the whole house smelled like home.

Arroz con gandules — rice with pigeon peas — is the undisputed national dish of Puerto Rico. It shows up at every holiday table, every birthday party, every casual Tuesday when someone decides tonight's a gandules night. And every family swears theirs is the best. The secret? It's not a fancy technique. It's the caldero, the sofrito, and the patience to not lift that lid while the rice cooks.

When I moved to the mainland, I realized how many people had never tasted real arroz con gandules — or worse, had tried a watered-down version that missed the point entirely. No pegao. No depth. Just... yellow rice with beans. That's when I decided to write down every detail of my family's recipe, tested until it works every single time, in any kitchen, anywhere.

This arroz con gandules recipe gives you everything: the fragrant sofrito base, the earthy pigeon peas, the smoky depth from optional turkey necks, and that golden crust of pegao at the bottom — the part everyone fights over. Whether you're cooking for Nochebuena or just a Wednesday night dinner, this is the recipe that makes Puerto Rican food taste like Puerto Rican food.

I've included every substitution, every tip, and every shortcut for mainland cooks. You don't need to fly to San Juan to get this right. You need a caldero, some sofrito, and this recipe. That's it.

What You'll Need (Kitchen Tools)

These are the tools that make this recipe foolproof. Every one is a staple in a boricua kitchen.

  • Caldero (Cast Aluminum Pot) — The heavy-bottomed pot that creates pegao. This is non-negotiable. A regular pot won't give you that crust.
  • Pilón (Mortar & Pestle) — For pounding garlic and spices the old-school way. Abuela never used a food processor.
  • Reina de la Cocina Spoon — A long-handled wooden spoon for stirring rice without scratching your caldero.

Arroz con Gandules

Arroz con Gandules — El plato nacional de Puerto Rico

Prep: 15 min Cook: 45 min Total: 60 min Servings: 8

Ingredients

  • 2 cups long-grain white rice
  • 2 cans (15 oz) pigeon peas, drained
  • 3 tablespoons sofrito
  • 2 packets sazón con culantro y achiote
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 2 cups chicken broth
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon dried oregano
  • 2 bay leaves
  • ½ cup alcaparrado (olives with pimientos)
  • 4 oz smoked turkey neck (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat olive oil in a 4-quart caldero over medium heat. If using turkey necks, brown 3 minutes per side, then push to the side.
  2. Add sofrito and sauté 2–3 minutes until fragrant. Stir in tomato paste and cook 1 minute.
  3. Add rice. Stir constantly for 2 minutes to coat every grain. Rice should look slightly translucent.
  4. Add pigeon peas, sazón, salt, oregano, bay leaves, and olives. Stir to combine.
  5. Pour in chicken broth. Stir once. Bring to a rolling boil over high heat.
  6. Reduce to lowest heat. Cover tightly. Cook 25 minutes — do NOT lift the lid.
  7. Remove from heat. Rest covered 10 minutes. Fluff with fork. Remove bay leaves.
  8. Scrape the bottom for pegao — the crispy rice crust. Serve hot.
👨‍🍳 Chef's Notes:
  • The pegao is the prize. Use a heavy caldero and don't stir the bottom. When you scrape it up at the end, you'll understand why people fight over it.
  • Rinse your rice. Rinse under cold water until the water runs clear. This removes excess starch and prevents mushy rice.
  • Sazón matters. Goya sazón con culantro y achiote is the gold standard. If you can't find it, see substitutions below.
  • Don't skip the sofrito. This is what makes it Puerto Rican rice and not just yellow rice. Homemade is best — here's our sofrito recipe.
Nutrition (per serving): 380 cal · 12g protein · 58g carbs · 10g fat · 4g fiber · 680mg sodium

Mainland US Substitutions

Can't find the island ingredients? Here's what works on the mainland:

  • Pigeon peas (gandules): Canned Goya gandules are available at most Walmart, Target, and Publix in the international aisle. Fresh or frozen gandules at Latin markets are even better.
  • Sofrito: Goya brand sofrito in the jar works in a pinch, but homemade is 10x better. Make our recipe here.
  • Sazón: Goya sazón is at every major grocery store. If unavailable, mix 1 tsp turmeric + ½ tsp garlic powder + pinch of annatto powder.
  • Alcaparrado: Goya alcaparrado (manzanilla olives with pimientos) is in the canned vegetable aisle. In a pinch, use ¼ cup green olives + 2 tablespoons pimientos.
  • Smoked turkey: Ham hocks, smoked turkey wings, or even bacon work. The point is smoky depth — use what you can find.
👵 Abuela's Tip: "El arroz no se mueve." — Don't stir the rice. Once you pour in the broth and lower the heat, that lid stays on. Trust the pot. The rice knows what to do. And when you hear that slight crackle at the bottom? That's the pegao forming. Don't touch it."

Frequently Asked Questions

Gandules are pigeon peas — a small, earthy legume that's a staple in Puerto Rican cooking. Canned gandules work perfectly for this recipe and are available at most grocery stores in the international aisle. Fresh or frozen gandules are even better if you can find them at a Latin market.
Pegao (pronounced peh-GOW) is the crispy, caramelized rice that forms on the bottom of the caldero. It's the most prized part of arroz con gandules — crunchy, golden, and packed with flavor. The secret is a heavy caldero, low heat, and not stirring the bottom layer.
You can, but it won't taste the same. Sazón con culantro y achiote gives the rice its signature orange color and earthy flavor. If you can't find it, mix 1 teaspoon turmeric, ½ teaspoon garlic powder, and a pinch of annatto powder as a substitute.
