How to Make Mofongo — Authentic Puerto Rican Recipe

Garlic-mashed green plantains with chicharrón, olive oil, and broth — Puerto Rico's most iconic comfort food.

⏱ 35 min 🍽 4 servings

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·11 min read

Jump to Recipe

There's a moment when you bite into proper mofongo — that first crunch of garlic and chicharrón giving way to the dense, pillowy mash of plantain — when you understand why Puerto Ricans call it their national dish of the soul. Mofongo isn't fancy. It isn't plated with tweezers. It's served in a pilón, still warm, with a little pool of broth around the base and maybe some garlic shrimp on top. It's honest food.

Every Puerto Rican has a mofongo story. Mine is from a roadside kiosk in Piñones, where an abuela with forearms like tree trunks was pounding plantains in a wooden mortar so fast it sounded like a drum solo. She'd fry the plantains, toss them in the pilón with a fistful of garlic and a handful of crushed chicharrón, and pound it all together until it formed a perfect dome. She'd hand it to you still steaming, with a little cup of caldo on the side. Three bites. Gone. Changed forever.

Learning how to make mofongo at home seemed impossible after that. But here's the truth: it's one of the simplest dishes in Puerto Rican cuisine. Green plantains, garlic, chicharrón, olive oil, broth. That's it. The technique matters more than the ingredients, and the technique is just: fry, mash, eat.

I spent years perfecting this recipe for mainland kitchens. Finding green plantains at a regular grocery store, substituting store-bought chicharrón when my cousin wasn't around to make it fresh, and figuring out how to get that texture right without a 40-year-old pilón. This recipe works. Every time. And once you master it, you're one step away from mofongo relleno — the stuffed version that's the crown jewel of Puerto Rican restaurants.

What You'll Need (Kitchen Tools)

  • Pilón (Wooden Mortar & Pestle) — The traditional tool for mofongo. A wooden pilón absorbs garlic oil and adds flavor over time. This is the one tool you can't substitute without losing something.
  • Deep Fry Thermometer — Green plantains need 350°F oil. Too hot and they burn outside while raw inside. Too cold and they absorb grease.
  • Spider Strainer — For safely lowering and removing plantains from hot oil. A slotted spoon works but a spider is better.
  • Reina de la Cocina Spoon — For serving and folding the mofongo. A sturdy wooden spoon is essential.

Mofongo

Mofongo — Plátanos majados con chicharrón

Prep: 15 min Cook: 20 min Total: 35 min Servings: 4

Ingredients

  • 4 green plantains
  • 6 cloves garlic, peeled
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 cup chicharrón, crushed
  • 1 cup chicken broth, warm
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • Vegetable oil for frying

Instructions

  1. Peel the plantains. Cut off both ends. Score the skin lengthwise in 3-4 places. Pry off with your thumb. Cut each plantain into 1-inch rounds.
  2. Fry at 350°F. Heat 2 inches of oil to 350°F. Fry plantain rounds 10-12 minutes, turning once, until golden. They should be tender inside but hold their shape.
  3. Pound the garlic base. While plantains fry, add garlic, salt, and pepper to a large pilón. Pound into a rough paste.
  4. Mash the plantains. Add hot fried plantains to the pilón in batches. Mash vigorously, adding warm broth a splash at a time, until chunky-smooth.
  5. Fold in the good stuff. Add crushed chicharrón, olive oil, and butter. Mash a few more times. Taste and adjust salt.
  6. Serve immediately. Mold into a dome on a plate or serve right in the pilón. Top with extra chicharrón. Serve with garlic shrimp or pernil.
👨‍🍳 Chef's Notes:
  • Green plantains only. Yellow plantains are too sweet and soft. You need the starchy, firm green ones for proper mofongo.
  • Work fast. Mofongo waits for no one. Mash while the plantains are hot — cold plantains won't mash properly.
  • The chicharrón matters. Use freshly crushed pork chicharrón, not the bagged snack kind. If you can't find fresh, Goya brand works.
  • Don't over-mash. Mofongo should be chunky-smooth, not a puree. You want texture — bits of plantain and chicharrón in every bite.
Nutrition (per serving): 480 cal · 14g protein · 52g carbs · 24g fat · 6g fiber · 590mg sodium

Mainland US Substitutions

  • Green plantains: Available at Walmart, Target, Kroger, and any Latin grocery store. Look for completely green, firm plantains. If they have any yellow, they're too ripe for mofongo.
  • Chicharrón: Goya chicharrón de puerco (pork cracklings) is at most grocery stores in the international aisle. In a pinch, use bacon bits — not ideal, but it works.
  • Pilón substitute: A large mortar and pestle, a potato masher, or a food processor (pulse only — never puree). The texture will be slightly different but still delicious.
  • Chicken broth: Any store-bought or homemade chicken broth works. Low-sodium is best so you can control the salt level.
👵 Abuela's Tip: "El mofongo no espera." — Mofongo doesn't wait. The moment those plantains come out of the oil, they go into the pilón. Don't let them cool. Don't check your phone. Mash while it's hot, serve while it's steaming. That's the only way."
