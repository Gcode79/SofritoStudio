There's a smell that means Sunday morning in every Puerto Rican household — and it's not coffee. It's butter hitting a warm pan, sugar caramelizing on a countertop, and the yeasty sweetness of **mallorcas** (mal-LOR-kas) rising from abuela's kitchen. If you grew up boricua — or married into a boricua family — you know exactly what I'm talking about.

My abuela Ortiz made mallorcas every Sunday without fail. She never used a recipe. Flour, yeast, sugar, butter, eggs — all measured by hand, all perfect every time. The trick wasn't in the ingredients; it was in the patience. She'd let the dough rise twice, sometimes three times, while the rest of the house slowly woke up to the smell. By the time the coffee was ready, the mallorcas were golden, soft, and rolled in sugar while still warm.

On the island, you grab mallorcas from any panadería before 8 a.m. They're the Puerto Rican answer to the croissant — except softer, sweeter, and coated in a layer of sugar that sticks to your fingers. Back home, every bakery has its version. Some add guava. Some stuff them with cream cheese. But the classic? Just soft, pillowy dough, brushed with butter, and rolled in granulated sugar. That's the one.

When I moved to the mainland, I couldn't find mallorcas anywhere. Panaderías that called themselves "Latin bakeries" had conchas and cuernos, but no mallorcas. So I called abuela. She walked me through it over the phone — "Josh, don't rush the yeast. Josh, more butter. Josh, the sugar has to be warm when you roll them." It took three tries, but by batch three, my kitchen smelled like Sunday morning again.

This **mallorcas recipe** uses ingredients you can find at any American grocery store. The only Puerto Rican-specific ingredient is the technique — the double rise, the butter bath, and the sugar roll that makes a mallorca a mallorca. If you have a stand mixer with a dough hook, even better. If not, your hands work fine. Abuela didn't have a KitchenAid.

This recipe makes 12 mallorcas — enough for the family and maybe a few left over (if you're lucky). They're best eaten the day they're made, while still warm. But honestly? They're pretty incredible the next morning, reheated for 10 seconds in the microwave.

Let's make mallorcas. *Vamos a hacer mallorcas.*

### Ingredients

**For the dough:**
- 1 cup whole milk, warm (110°F / 43°C)
- 2¼ teaspoons (1 packet) instant yeast
- ¼ cup granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs, room temperature
- ½ cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 3½ to 4 cups all-purpose flour

**For the butter wash:**
- ¼ cup (½ stick) unsalted butter, melted

**For coating:**
- ¾ cup granulated sugar

---

### Instructions

1. **Activate the yeast.** Pour warm milk into a large bowl (or the bowl of a stand mixer). Sprinkle the yeast and a pinch of sugar over the surface. Stir gently and let sit for 5–10 minutes until foamy. If it doesn't foam, your yeast is dead — start over with fresh yeast and warmer (not hot) milk.

2. **Build the dough.** Add the sugar, eggs, melted butter, vanilla, and salt to the yeast mixture. Whisk to combine. Add 3½ cups of flour and stir with a wooden spoon (or use the dough hook on low speed) until a shaggy dough forms.

3. **Knead.** Turn dough out onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 8–10 minutes, adding flour a tablespoon at a time if sticky. The dough should be smooth, slightly tacky, and spring back when poked. If using a stand mixer, knead on medium-low for 6–7 minutes with the dough hook.

4. **First rise.** Place dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap, and let rise in a warm spot for 1 to 1½ hours, until doubled in size. *(Abuela's tip: "Put it near the stove if the oven is on. Heat helps it rise faster.")*

5. **Shape the rolls.** Punch down the dough and turn it out onto a lightly floured surface. Divide into 12 equal pieces (a kitchen scale helps — each piece should be about 65–70g). Roll each piece into a smooth ball by cupping your hand over it and moving in a circular motion on the counter.

6. **Second rise.** Place the shaped rolls on a parchment-lined baking sheet, spaced about 2 inches apart. Cover loosely and let rise for 30–45 minutes until puffy and nearly doubled.

7. **Bake.** Preheat oven to 350°F (175°C). Bake for 12–15 minutes until golden brown on top. They should sound slightly hollow when tapped on the bottom.

8. **Butter and sugar.** While still warm, brush each roll generously with melted butter, then roll in granulated sugar, coating all sides. The sugar should stick to the butter — don't be shy with either.

9. **Serve immediately.** Mallorcas are best eaten warm, the same day. Pair with café con leche for the full boricua breakfast experience.

---

### Chef's Notes & Abuela Tips

- **"No te apures con el yeast"** — Don't rush the yeast. If your kitchen is cold, the rise takes longer. Be patient. Under-risen dough = dense rolls.
- **Milk temperature matters.** Too hot (above 120°F) kills the yeast. Too cold and it won't activate. Aim for 105–110°F — warm to the touch, not hot.
- **Butter quality matters.** Use real butter, not margarine. This is not the recipe to save 50 cents.
- **Freeze before baking.** Shape the rolls, place on a baking sheet, and freeze until solid. Transfer to a freezer bag. To bake: thaw overnight in the fridge, then let rise 1 hour at room temp before baking. Fresh mallorcas on a Tuesday morning.
- **Variations:** Add 2 tablespoons of cocoa powder to the dough for chocolate mallorcas. Or fill with guava paste before shaping for *mallorcas de guayaba*.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between mallorcas and regular sweet rolls?**
Mallorcas are a Puerto Rican variation of the Spanish ensaïmada. They're softer and richer than American sweet rolls because of the high butter and egg content in the dough. The signature feature is the butter-and-sugar coating applied while still warm — regular sweet rolls are typically glazed with icing instead.

**Can I make mallorcas ahead of time?**
Yes. You can shape the rolls and do the second rise, then refrigerate them overnight (covered tightly). In the morning, let them come to room temperature for 30–45 minutes, then bake as directed. You can also freeze shaped, unbaked rolls for up to 3 months — thaw overnight in the fridge, rise at room temperature, then bake.

**Why didn't my mallorcas rise?**
The most common cause is dead or expired yeast. Check the expiration date on your yeast packet. Also, make sure your milk was warm (105–110°F), not hot — temperatures above 120°F kill yeast. If your kitchen is cold, try placing the dough in a slightly warm oven (turned off, with just the light on) to create a warm rising environment.

**Can I use bread flour instead of all-purpose flour?**
Yes. Bread flour has more protein, which creates more gluten and gives the mallorcas a chewier texture. All-purpose flour gives a softer, more tender crumb. Both work — it's a matter of preference. If using bread flour, you may need slightly less (start with 3¼ cups).

**What goes well with mallorcas?**
Café con leche is the classic pairing — strong Puerto Rican coffee with steamed milk. They're also great with a side of scrambled eggs and salchichas (Puerto Rican sausages) for a full Boricua breakfast. See more in our [Boricua Breakfasts](/blog.html#boricua-breakfasts) collection.
