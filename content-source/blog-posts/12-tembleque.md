If there's one dessert that defines a Puerto Rican celebration, it's **tembleque** (tem-BLEH-keh). The name literally means "trembling" — and once you unmold this silky coconut pudding, you'll see why. It jiggles like it's dancing. Every Puerto Rican kid has poked a tembleque with a spoon just to watch it wobble.

My abuela Ortiz made tembleque for every holiday. Christmas, Easter, birthdays, random Tuesdays — if there was a reason to celebrate (or even if there wasn't), tembleque appeared on the table. She made it in a round copper mold that she'd had since the 1970s. When she unmolded it onto her best plate, the whole family went quiet for a second. Then the spoons came out.

What makes tembleque different from other coconut puddings? Two things: **cornstarch** (not gelatin) and **tradition**. The cornstarch gives it that distinctive dense-yet-wobbly texture — firmer than panna cotta, softer than flan. And the tradition? That's the part no recipe can teach you. It's the reason you make it. For family. For the holidays. For the memory of abuela's kitchen.

The good news: tembleque is one of the easiest Puerto Rican desserts you can make. **No baking. No eggs. No fancy equipment.** Coconut milk, sugar, cornstarch, and salt — that's it. You cook it on the stovetop, pour it into a mold, and chill it until set. Unmold, dust with cinnamon, and serve.

This **tembleque recipe** uses canned coconut milk, which you can find at any grocery store on the mainland. Full-fat coconut milk is essential — the "light" version won't set properly. The starch-to-liquid ratio in this recipe has been tested dozens of times (ask my wife) to get the perfect texture: firm enough to unmold cleanly, soft enough to melt on your tongue.

Total active time is about 15 minutes. The rest is chilling. Make it the day before your gathering and you're ahead of the game.

Let's make tembleque. *Vamos a hacer tembleque.*

### Ingredients

- 2 cans (13.5 oz each) full-fat coconut milk
- ¾ cup granulated sugar
- ¼ cup cornstarch
- ¼ teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- Ground cinnamon, for dusting
- Toasted coconut flakes, for garnish (optional)

---

### Instructions

1. **Dissolve the cornstarch.** Pour one can of coconut milk into a medium saucepan. Add the cornstarch and whisk vigorously until completely dissolved — no lumps. This is your slurry.

2. **Add everything else.** Pour in the second can of coconut milk, sugar, and salt. Whisk to combine. Place the saucepan over medium heat.

3. **Cook, stirring constantly.** Stir continuously with a silicone whisk or wooden spoon. The mixture will thin out at first, then gradually thicken. After 6–8 minutes, it should coat the back of a spoon and hold a trail when you drag your finger through it. When it starts to bubble gently, it's done. *(Abuela's tip: "Don't walk away. Cornstarch burns fast.")*

4. **Add vanilla.** Remove from heat and stir in the vanilla extract. The mixture should be the consistency of thick pudding.

5. **Pour into mold.** Lightly grease your mold with coconut oil or cooking spray. Pour the hot tembleque mixture into the mold. Tap gently on the counter to release any air bubbles. Smooth the top with a spatula.

6. **Chill.** Let cool at room temperature for 20 minutes, then cover with plastic wrap (press it directly onto the surface to prevent a skin from forming) and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or overnight.

7. **Unmold.** Run a thin knife around the edges of the mold. Place your serving plate on top and flip quickly — one confident motion. The tembleque should release cleanly. If it sticks, dip the bottom of the mold in warm water for 10 seconds and try again.

8. **Garnish and serve.** Dust generously with ground cinnamon. Top with toasted coconut flakes if desired. Slice or scoop and serve cold.

### Chef's Notes & Abuela Tips

- **"No uses leche de coco light"** — Use full-fat coconut milk. The lite version has too much water and won't set firmly. This is the one time "healthy" doesn't help.
- **Cornstarch, not gelatin.** This is what makes tembleque Puerto Rican. Gelatin gives a different, bouncier texture. Cornstarch gives the dense, wobbly, creamy texture that's unique to this dessert.
- **Make it the day before.** Tembleque needs at least 4 hours to set, but overnight is better. It firms up more and unmolds more cleanly.
- **Variations:** Add 2 tablespoons of cocoa powder to make chocolate tembleque. Or swirl in ¼ cup of guava paste (melted) for a guava-coconut version. Some families add a drop of coconut extract for extra intensity.
- **Mold matters.** Traditional Puerto Rican molds are round and fluted. If you don't have one, a regular 8-inch round cake pan, a bundt pan, or even individual ramekins work great. Silicone molds unmold the easiest.
- **The cinnamon is not optional.** It's not just decoration — the warm cinnamon against the cool coconut is the flavor contrast that makes tembleque work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why didn't my tembleque set?**
The most common reasons are: (1) you used light coconut milk instead of full-fat, (2) you didn't cook it long enough — it needs to reach a thick, bubbling consistency before you remove it from heat, or (3) you didn't chill it long enough. It needs a minimum of 4 hours, but overnight is ideal. If it still won't set after overnight chilling, you can re-heat it, add a tablespoon more cornstarch dissolved in a splash of coconut milk, cook until thick, and re-chill.

**Can I make tembleque dairy-free?**
Tembleque is naturally dairy-free — it's made with coconut milk, cornstarch, sugar, and salt. No cream, no eggs, no butter. It's one of the most allergy-friendly Puerto Rican desserts you can make. Just check that your vanilla extract is alcohol-based, not cream-based.

**How far in advance can I make tembleque?**
Tembleque keeps well in the refrigerator for up to 4 days, covered tightly. It's actually better on day 2 — the texture firms up and the flavors meld. Do not freeze it; the texture breaks down. Make it 1–2 days before your event for the best results.

**What's the difference between tembleque and flan?**
Flan is an egg-custard dessert baked in a caramel-lined mold (bain-marie). Tembleque is a coconut pudding thickened with cornstarch — no eggs, no baking. Flan is creamy and dense; tembleque is lighter, wobblier, and coconut-forward. Both are Puerto Rican classics, but they're completely different desserts.

**Can I add flavorings to tembleque?**
Absolutely. Popular variations include chocolate tembleque (add cocoa powder), coconut-lime (add lime zest), and pandan tembleque (add pandan extract for a bright green version popular in some Caribbean households). See more in our [Postres Boricuas](/blog.html#postres-boricuas) collection.
