Walk down any street in San Juan, and you'll smell them before you see them: **empanadillas** (em-pah-nee-DEE-yas) — golden, crescent-shaped turnovers stuffed with seasoned beef, deep-fried until the shell shatters when you bite into them. They're Puerto Rican street food at its finest. The kind of food you eat standing up, with hot sauce dripping down your wrist, not caring who sees.

Every boricua has an empanadilla story. Mine involves my Tío Rafael and a kiosk in Rincón. Every summer, he'd take me to this beachside spot where an abuela ran a frying station with three enormous pots of oil. You'd order, she'd drop the empanadillas in, and three minutes later you'd be eating the best thing you'd ever tasted. She made the dough from scratch — you could see her rolling it out behind the counter. "La masa lo es todo," she told me once. The dough is everything. She was right.

The word "empanadilla" is the diminutive of "empanada" — it literally means "little empanada." In Puerto Rico, the terms are used almost interchangeably, but empanadillas tend to be smaller, thinner, and more delicate than the large, thick-crust empanadas you find in other Latin American countries. The dough is thinner, the filling is spiced with sofrito and sazón, and the edges are sealed with a fork — that crimped edge is a signature.

The **filling** is where Puerto Rican empanadillas differ from every other version. The base is ground beef (or sometimes pork), cooked with sofrito, tomato sauce, olives, and capers. It's savory, slightly briny, and deeply seasoned. This isn't a bland ground beef turnover — this is a flavor bomb wrapped in crispy dough.

This **empanadillas recipe** makes 16 turnovers — enough for a party, a game day spread, or a week of lunches (they reheat beautifully in the oven). The dough uses just 5 ingredients: flour, butter, salt, sugar, and ice water. The filling takes about 15 minutes. Assembly takes another 20. Total time from start to eating: about 1 hour.

The secret to crispy empanadillas? **Ice-cold water in the dough, and hot oil for frying.** If the water is warm, the butter melts and the dough gets tough. If the oil isn't hot enough, the dough absorbs oil and gets greasy. Get those two things right, and you're golden — literally.

Let's make empanadillas. *Vamos a hacer empanadillas.*

### Ingredients

**For the dough:**
- 3 cups all-purpose flour
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon granulated sugar
- ½ cup (1 stick) cold unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
- ¾ cup ice water (add gradually — you may not need all of it)

**For the filling:**
- 1 lb ground beef (or ground pork, or a mix)
- 3 tablespoons sofrito
- 1 packet sazón con culantro y achiote
- ¼ cup tomato sauce
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- ¼ cup manzanilla olives, chopped (with some brine)
- 2 tablespoons capers, drained
- ½ teaspoon dried oregano
- ½ teaspoon cumin
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 2 tablespoons olive oil

**For frying:**
- Vegetable or canola oil (about 4 cups, enough for 3 inches of depth)

---

### Instructions

**Make the dough:**

1. **Combine dry ingredients.** In a large bowl, whisk together flour, salt, and sugar.

2. **Cut in the butter.** Add the cold butter cubes. Use a pastry cutter, two forks, or your fingers to work the butter into the flour until it resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces remaining. *(Abuela's tip: "Keep the butter cold. If it melts, the dough gets tough.")*

3. **Add ice water.** Drizzle in the ice water, 2 tablespoons at a time, mixing gently with a fork after each addition. Stop when the dough just comes together — it should hold when pressed but not be sticky. You may not need all the water.

4. **Form and chill.** Shape the dough into a disc, wrap in plastic, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes. *(This relaxes the gluten and keeps the butter cold — both matter.)*

**Make the filling:**

5. **Brown the beef.** Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and break it up with a spoon. Cook until browned, about 5 minutes. Drain excess fat.

6. **Season.** Add the sofrito and cook for 2 minutes until fragrant. Add the sazón, tomato sauce, tomato paste, oregano, and cumin. Stir well. Add ¼ cup water and let simmer for 8–10 minutes until the sauce thickens.

7. **Finish.** Stir in the chopped olives and capers (plus a splash of olive brine for extra flavor). Season with salt and pepper. Transfer to a bowl and let cool completely before filling. *(Hot filling = soggy dough.)*

**Assemble:**

8. **Roll and cut.** On a lightly floured surface, roll the dough to about ⅛ inch thick. Cut into 4-inch circles using a round cutter, bowl, or the top of a glass.

9. **Fill.** Place about 1½ tablespoons of filling in the center of each circle. Don't overfill — leave enough edge to seal.

10. **Seal.** Fold the dough over the filling to form a half-moon. Press the edges together with your fingers, then crimp with a fork to seal tightly. *(A bad seal = filling leaking into the oil = disaster.)*

**Fry:**

11. **Heat oil.** In a deep pot or Dutch oven, heat oil to 350°F (175°C). Use a thermometer — don't guess.

12. **Fry in batches.** Carefully lower 3–4 empanadillas into the oil. Don't crowd the pot — the temperature will drop and they'll get greasy. Fry for 2–3 minutes per side until deep golden brown.

13. **Drain.** Transfer to a wire rack set over a paper towel-lined sheet pan. Sprinkle with a pinch of salt while hot. Let cool for 5 minutes before eating — the filling is volcanic.

---

### Chef's Notes & Abuela Tips

- **"La masa tiene que estar fría"** — The dough must stay cold. If the butter warms up, you lose flakiness. Work quickly and keep returning the dough to the fridge between batches.
- **Filling must be cold.** This is the #1 mistake. Warm filling melts the butter in the dough and makes it impossible to seal. Make the filling first, let it cool completely, then roll the dough.
- **Baked option:** Preheat oven to 400°F. Place assembled empanadillas on a parchment-lined baking sheet, brush with egg wash (1 egg + 1 tablespoon water), and bake for 18–22 minutes until golden. Less authentic but less messy.
- **Freeze for later.** Assemble the empanadillas and freeze on a sheet pan until solid. Transfer to a freezer bag. To cook: fry directly from frozen (add 1–2 minutes to cook time) or bake from frozen at 375°F for 25 minutes.
- **Filling variations:** Swap beef for shredded chicken (pollo guisado style), cheese and onion, or picadillo with raisins and capers. The dough is a blank canvas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between empanadillas and empanadas?**
In Puerto Rico, the terms are used almost interchangeably. Empanadillas tend to be smaller (4-inch diameter), thinner dough, and crimped with a fork. Larger empanadas (6+ inches) may have a thicker, hand-formed edge. In other Latin American countries, the distinction is more pronounced — but in Puerto Rico, it's mostly about size.

**Can I bake empanadillas instead of frying?**
Yes. Preheat oven to 400°F, brush with egg wash, and bake for 18–22 minutes until golden. The texture will be different — more like a baked turnover than a fried pastry — but they're still delicious and much less messy. Some families actually prefer the baked version for everyday eating.

**How do I keep empanadillas crispy?**
Place them on a wire rack (not a plate) after frying. Air circulation prevents steam from softening the bottom. If making ahead, reheat in a 350°F oven for 5–7 minutes — never the microwave, which makes them soggy.

**Can I freeze uncooked empanadillas?**
Absolutely. Assemble them, place on a parchment-lined sheet pan, and freeze until solid (about 2 hours). Transfer to a freezer bag. They keep for up to 3 months. Fry or bake directly from frozen — just add 1–2 extra minutes to the cook time.

**What dipping sauce goes with empanadillas?**
Mojo isleño (garlic sauce) is the classic — garlic, olive oil, vinegar, and a little heat. Ketchup-mayo mix (salsa rosada) is also popular, especially for kids. Hot sauce (pique or Tabasco) works great too. See more street food recipes in our [Comida Callejera](/blog.html#comida-callejera) collection.
