There's a reason **arroz con pollo** (chicken with rice) is one of the most-searched Puerto Rican recipes in the world. It's the dish that proves Puerto Rican cooking isn't complicated — it's just deeply seasoned, patient, and made with love. One pot. One meal. Enough to feed a crowd.

Every Puerto Rican family has their version. Some add peas. Some add olives. Some use bone-in thighs, some use boneless. But every version starts with the same two things: **sofrito and sazón.** Without those, it's just chicken and rice. With them, it's Puerto Rico on a plate.

My abuela Ortiz made arroz con pollo at least twice a month. It was her "I don't feel like cooking" recipe — which is ironic, because it tastes like it took all day. The truth is, it takes about an hour, and most of that is hands-off. The rice absorbs all the flavor from the chicken, the sofrito, and the sazón, turning that beautiful golden-orange color that's unmistakable. When she lifted the lid off that caldero and the steam hit your face — that was the signal.

The technique matters here. Puerto Rican arroz con pollo isn't a casserole. It's not a bake. It's a **stovetop one-pot meal** where the chicken is browned first, the sofrito is sautéed, and the rice is cooked right in the same pot, absorbing every drop of flavor. The result is rice that's seasoned all the way through — not white rice with chicken on top.

The biggest mistake mainland cooks make? **Lifting the lid.** Once the rice is in and the heat is low, that lid stays on. No peeking. No stirring. The steam does the work. Abuela used to say: "Si levantas la tapa, se te arruina el arroz." If you lift the lid, you ruin the rice. She wasn't kidding.

This **arroz con pollo puerto rican** recipe serves 6–8 people and uses ingredients you can find at any mainland grocery store. The only "special" ingredient is sazón — those little packets of seasoning that give Puerto Rican rice its signature color and flavor. Find them in the Latin aisle at Walmart, Target, Publix, or any grocery store.

Let's cook. *Vamos a cocinar.*

### Ingredients

- 3 lbs bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (8–10 pieces)
- 2 cups long-grain white rice
- 3 tablespoons sofrito
- 2 packets sazón con culantro y achiote
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 1 cup frozen peas (optional, but traditional)
- ½ cup manzanilla olives (with a splash of brine)
- ¼ cup alcaparrado (capers and olives mix) — optional
- 3 cups chicken broth (low-sodium preferred)
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste)
- ½ teaspoon dried oregano
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- ½ teaspoon black pepper

---

### Instructions

1. **Season the chicken.** Pat chicken dry with paper towels. Season generously with salt, pepper, and garlic powder on both sides. *(Dry skin = crispy skin. Wet skin = steamed chicken.)*

2. **Brown the chicken.** Heat olive oil in a caldero or heavy pot over medium-high heat. Place chicken skin-side down and cook for 5–6 minutes per side until golden brown. Work in batches — don't crowd the pot. Remove and set aside. *(You're not cooking it through — just building flavor on the skin.)*

3. **Build the base.** In the same pot (keep the chicken fat), add the sofrito. Sauté for 2–3 minutes until fragrant and slightly caramelized. Add the tomato paste and stir for 1 minute — this deepens the flavor.

4. **Toast the rice.** Add the rice to the pot and stir to coat every grain in the sofrito oil. Cook for 2 minutes, stirring frequently. The rice should look slightly translucent at the edges.

5. **Add liquid and seasonings.** Pour in the chicken broth. Add the sazón packets, oregano, bay leaves, olives, and alcaparrado (if using). Stir once to combine.

6. **Nestle the chicken.** Place the browned chicken pieces skin-side up on top of the rice. Press them down slightly so they're half-submerged. Scatter the frozen peas around the chicken.

7. **Bring to a boil, then reduce.** Increase heat to high and bring to a boil. As soon as it boils, reduce heat to the lowest setting. Cover tightly. **DO NOT LIFT THE LID.**

8. **Cook for 25 minutes.** Set your timer. Walk away. Do not stir. Do not peek. Do not adjust the heat. The steam and the low heat do all the work.

9. **Rest.** After 25 minutes, remove from heat. Let rest, still covered, for 10 minutes. *(This finishes the cooking and lets the rice absorb any remaining moisture.)*

10. **Serve.** Remove bay leaves. Fluff the rice gently around the chicken. Serve directly from the pot — family style, the way abuela intended.

---

### Chef's Notes & Abuela Tips

- **"El arroz no se mece"** — Don't stir the rice once it's covered. Stirring releases steam and makes the rice gummy. Trust the process.
- **Bone-in, skin-on is non-negotiable.** The bones add flavor to the rice. The skin adds richness. If you must use boneless, add extra chicken broth (½ cup) because bone-in chicken releases more liquid.
- **Sazón is the secret.** Those Goya sazón packets (con culantro y achiote) give the rice that golden-orange color and signature flavor. There's no substitute. They're at every grocery store on the mainland.
- **The pegao is the best part.** That crispy, caramelized rice at the bottom of the pot? That's pegao (peh-GOW). Some families fight over it. When you scrape the bottom at serving time, don't be gentle.
- **Make it ahead.** This reheats beautifully. Store in the fridge for up to 4 days. Add a splash of water when reheating to refresh the rice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is my rice mushy?**
The most common causes: (1) you lifted the lid while it was cooking, releasing steam; (2) you stirred the rice after adding the liquid; (3) you used too much broth; or (4) the heat was too high. The ratio is 2 cups rice to 3 cups liquid. The heat should be the lowest your burner can go once it reaches a boil.

**Can I use boneless chicken thighs or breast?**
You can, but bone-in, skin-on thighs give the best flavor. If using boneless, reduce cook time to 20 minutes (no lid-lifting!) and use 2½ cups broth instead of 3. Breast works but tends to dry out — thighs are more forgiving.

**What is sazón and where do I find it?**
Sazón is a Puerto Rican seasoning blend made with coriander, cumin, achiote (annatto), garlic, and other spices. The packets are sold in the Latin/Hispanic aisle of most grocery stores (Walmart, Target, Publix, Kroger). Goya is the most common brand. It's what gives Puerto Rican rice its signature golden-orange color.

**Can I make arroz con pollo in a rice cooker?**
Partially. Brown the chicken and sauté the sofrito in a skillet, then transfer everything to a rice cooker. Use the chicken broth as your liquid. The result won't have the same depth of flavor as a stovetop caldero, but it works in a pinch.

**What side dishes go with arroz con pollo?**
Habichuelas guisadas (stewed beans) is the classic pairing. A simple ensalada verde (green salad) with avocado rounds it out. See more in our [La Mesa Boricua](/blog.html#la-mesa-boricua) collection.
