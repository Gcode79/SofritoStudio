In Puerto Rico, the word **pinchos** (PEEN-chos) means skewers — but it means so much more than that. Pinchos are the island's ultimate street food. You'll find them at roadside kiosks, beach stands, and backyard parrilladas (grill parties) across the island. Chicken or pork, threaded onto sticks, grilled over charcoal, and glazed with a sweet-savory adobo sauce that caramelizes into a sticky, smoky coating.

Every boricua has strong opinions about pinchos. Chicken or pork? Bone-in or boneless? Wood sticks or metal? Sweet glaze or spicy? There's no single "right" answer — but there are a few non-negotiables. The marinade has to have adobo. The grill has to be hot. And the bread — yes, bread — has to be there to soak up the drippings.

My Tío Rafael made the best pinchos on the block. He marinated his chicken overnight in a mix of adobo, garlic, olive oil, and a splash of vinegar. Then he'd thread them onto wooden sticks (soaked in water to prevent burning) and grill them over charcoal until the edges were charred and the glaze was bubbling. He always served them with a slice of crusty bread on the stick, right at the end — "para limpiar el plato," he'd say. To clean the plate. The bread absorbed all the drippings. It was the best bite on the stick.

The **glaze** is what separates Puerto Rican pinchos from regular grilled chicken skewers. It's a reduction of adobo seasoning, ketchup, garlic, and a touch of sugar — cooked down until it's thick and sticky. You brush it on during the last few minutes of grilling, and it caramelizes into a lacquer that's sweet, savory, and slightly smoky. That glaze is everything.

This **pinchos recipe** works with chicken or pork (or both). The marinade takes 5 minutes. The grilling takes about 10. Total time: under 20 minutes of active work, plus marinating time. If you have a grill, use it — the charcoal flavor is essential. But a grill pan or even the broiler works in a pinch.

Let's grill. *Vamos a asar.*

**Prep Time:** 15 minutes (+ 1 hour marinating) | **Cook Time:** 10 minutes | **Total Time:** 25 minutes (+ marinating)
**Servings:** 6–8 skewers | **Difficulty:** Easy

---

### Ingredients

**For the marinade:**
- 2 lbs boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch cubes
- 3 tablespoons adobo seasoning (Goya brand)
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons fresh lime juice
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- ½ teaspoon black pepper

**For the glaze:**
- ¼ cup ketchup
- 2 tablespoons adobo seasoning
- 1 tablespoon honey or brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- Pinch of cayenne (optional — for heat)

**For serving:**
- Cuban bread or French bread, sliced (for the stick)
- Lime wedges
- Pique (Puerto Rican hot sauce) or your favorite hot sauce

---

### Instructions

**Marinate the chicken:**

1. **Combine marinade.** In a large bowl, whisk together the adobo, olive oil, garlic, lime juice, oregano, and pepper.

2. **Marinate.** Add the chicken cubes and toss to coat. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or up to overnight. *(Overnight is ideal — the acid from the lime tenderizes the meat.)*

**Make the glaze:**

3. **Cook the glaze.** In a small saucepan over medium heat, combine ketchup, adobo, honey, vinegar, garlic, olive oil, and cayenne (if using). Stir and bring to a gentle simmer. Cook for 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened. Remove from heat and set aside.

**Grill:**

4. **Preheat grill.** Heat your grill (outdoor charcoal, gas, or grill pan) to medium-high heat — about 400°F. Clean and oil the grates.

5. **Thread the chicken.** Remove chicken from the marinade. Thread 4–5 cubes onto each skewer, leaving a small gap between pieces for even cooking.

6. **Grill.** Place skewers on the grill. Cook for 3–4 minutes per side, turning once, until the chicken reaches 165°F internal and has nice char marks.

7. **Glaze.** During the last 2 minutes of grilling, brush the glaze generously onto each skewer. Flip and glaze the other side. The glaze should caramelize and get slightly sticky. *(Don't glaze too early — the sugar will burn.)*

8. **Optional: add bread.** Thread a slice of crusty bread onto the end of each skewer during the last minute. Let it toast slightly and absorb the dripping glaze. This is the Tío Rafael move.

9. **Serve.** Remove from grill. Squeeze lime over the top and serve immediately with pique on the side.

### Chef's Notes & Abuela Tips

- **"El adobo es la clave"** — Adobo seasoning is the backbone of Puerto Rican pinchos. Goya adobo is the standard — find it in the Latin aisle at any grocery store. There's also adobo with pepper, adobo with sazón, and low-sodium versions. Use the original.
- **Charcoal is king.** If you have a charcoal grill, use it. The smoky flavor from charcoal is what makes pinchos taste like the island. Gas grills work too, but you lose some of that depth.
- **Soak your bamboo skewers.** If using wooden skewers, soak them in water for at least 30 minutes before grilling. Dry bamboo catches fire on the grill. Metal skewers skip this step entirely.
- **Chicken thighs over breast.** Thighs stay juicy on the grill. Breast dries out fast, especially over high heat. If you must use breast, pound it to even thickness and don't overcook.
- **Make it a parrillada.** In Puerto Rico, pinchos are part of a larger grill spread. Add tostones, grilled corn, and a big pot of arroz con gandules for the full experience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is adobo seasoning?**
Adobo is a Puerto Rican all-purpose seasoning made with garlic, oregano, black pepper, and salt. Goya is the most common brand and is available in the Latin/Hispanic aisle of virtually every grocery store on the mainland. It comes in several varieties — the original (yellow lid) is the standard for pinchos. Some versions include sazón or pepper for extra flavor.

**Can I use pork instead of chicken?**
Absolutely. Pork pinchos (pinchos de cerdo) are equally popular on the island. Use boneless pork loin or shoulder, cut into 1-inch cubes. Marinate the same way. Pork takes slightly longer on the grill — aim for 145°F internal. The glaze works the same way on pork.

**What if I don't have an outdoor grill?**
A cast iron grill pan on the stovetop gives you char marks and smoky flavor. You can also use the broiler — set the oven to high, place skewers on a sheet pan 4 inches from the heat, and broil for 4–5 minutes per side. The char won't be as pronounced, but the flavor will still be great.

**Why do Puerto Ricans put bread on the skewer?**
The bread (usually Cuban or French bread) is placed at the end of the skewer during the last minute of grilling. It toasts slightly and absorbs the dripping glaze and chicken juices. It's essentially edible napkin — and it's delicious. Tío Rafael called it "the best bite on the stick."

**What's the difference between pinchos and brochettes?**
Pinchos are specifically Puerto Rican — seasoned with adobo, glazed with a sweet-savory sauce, and typically served with bread on the stick. Brochettes is the French term for any skewered grilled meat, with different seasonings and no glaze tradition. Both are delicious; pinchos are boricua.
