## EMAIL 3: DAY 2 — Cultural Primer

**Subject:** What is sofrito, really?
**Preview:** The flavor base that makes Puerto Rican food taste like Puerto Rican food

---

Every Puerto Rican dish starts with the same thing: a good base.

That base is sofrito.

Sofrito is a blend of culantro (recao), peppers, garlic, and herbs, sautéed into the flavor of home. It's the first thing that hits the pan. The aroma that fills the kitchen. The foundation that transforms rice, beans, and meats from ordinary to extraordinary.

**What's in traditional sofrito:**
- Culantro (recao) — the leafy herb that gives sofrito its distinctive flavor
- Ají dulce peppers — sweet, mild, essential
- Bell peppers — for sweetness and color
- Onions and garlic — the backbone
- Cilantro — for brightness
- Olive oil — to bind it all together

**Why it matters:**

When you cook Puerto Rican food without sofrito, it's like cooking Italian food without garlic. Technically possible, but missing the soul.

Sofrito is what makes arroz con gandules taste like arroz con gandules. It's what makes habichuelas guisadas taste like habichuelas guisadas. It's the difference between "rice and beans" and "Puerto Rican rice and beans."

**The good news:** You can make it at home in 15 minutes. And once you do, you can make any Puerto Rican dish.

## EMAIL 4: DAY 4 — Best Recipe

**Subject:** The recipe everyone asks for
**Preview:** Arroz con Gandules — the dish that made us famous

---

If there's one recipe that defines Puerto Rican cooking, it's arroz con gandules.

Rice with pigeon peas. The national dish. The thing every Puerto Rican family makes at least once a week — and argues about who makes it best.

Here's our version from the Ortiz kitchen:

### Arroz con Gandules

**Prep:** 15 min | **Cook:** 45 min | **Serves:** 6-8

**Ingredients:**
- 2 cups long-grain white rice
- 1 can (15 oz) pigeon peas, drained
- 3 tablespoons sofrito
- 2 packets sazón con culantro y achiote
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 2 cups chicken broth
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon oregano
- 2 bay leaves

**Instructions:**
1. Heat oil in a caldero or heavy pot over medium heat.
2. Add sofrito. Sauté for 2-3 minutes until fragrant.
3. Add tomato paste. Stir and cook for 1 minute.
4. Add rice. Stir to coat every grain. Cook for 2 minutes.
5. Add pigeon peas, sazón, salt, oregano, and bay leaves.
6. Pour in chicken broth. Stir once. Bring to a boil.
7. Reduce heat to LOW. Cover tightly. Cook for 25 minutes WITHOUT lifting the lid.
8. Remove from heat. Rest, covered, for 10 minutes.
9. Fluff with fork. Remove bay leaves. Serve hot.

**The secret:** Don't lift the lid. Trust the process. The rice steams itself to perfection.

**The pegao:** The crispy bottom layer is the best part. Use a caldero and don't stir the bottom. When you scrape it up at the end, you'll understand.
