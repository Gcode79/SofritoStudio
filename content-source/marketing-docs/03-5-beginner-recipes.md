## PAGE 2: INTRODUCTION

### Welcome to Puerto Rican Cooking

You don't need to be Puerto Rican to cook Puerto Rican food. You just need good ingredients, a little patience, and the willingness to taste as you go.

These 5 recipes are the foundation of Boricua cooking. Master these, and you can cook any Puerto Rican dish. They're the recipes I grew up with in the Ortiz kitchen — the ones abuela made every week without thinking.

**What You'll Learn:**
1. **Arroz con Gandules** — The national dish. Rice with pigeon peas.
2. **Habichuelas Guisadas** — Stewed beans. The comfort food of Puerto Rico.
3. **Tostones** — Fried green plantains. The perfect side dish.
4. **Flan de Queso** — Creamy cheese flan. The classic dessert.
5. **Café con Leche** — Puerto Rican coffee with milk. The morning ritual.

**Before You Start:**
- Read each recipe completely before cooking
- Prep all ingredients first (mise en place)
- Taste as you go — your palate is your best tool
- Don't rush. Puerto Rican food is about love, not speed

## PAGE 3-4: RECIPE 1 — ARROZ CON GANDULES

### Arroz con Gandules
*The National Dish of Puerto Rico*

**Prep:** 15 min | **Cook:** 45 min | **Serves:** 6-8

This is THE dish. Every Puerto Rican household makes it differently, but the base is always the same: rice, pigeon peas, sofrito, and sazón.

#### Ingredients

- 2 cups long-grain white rice
- 1 can (15 oz) pigeon peas (gandules), drained
- 3 tablespoons sofrito
- 2 packets sazón con culantro y achiote
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 2 cups chicken broth
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon oregano
- 2 bay leaves

#### Instructions

1. **Heat oil** in a caldero or heavy pot over medium heat.

2. **Add sofrito.** Sauté for 2-3 minutes until fragrant. This is the base of everything.

3. **Add tomato paste.** Stir and cook for 1 minute.

4. **Add rice.** Stir to coat every grain in the sofrito oil. Cook for 2 minutes.

5. **Add pigeon peas, sazón, salt, oregano, and bay leaves.** Stir well.

6. **Pour in chicken broth.** Stir once to combine. Bring to a boil.

7. **Reduce heat to LOW.** Cover tightly. Cook for 25 minutes WITHOUT lifting the lid.

8. **Remove from heat.** Let rest, covered, for 10 minutes.

9. **Fluff with fork.** Remove bay leaves. Serve hot.

#### Chef's Notes
- "The secret is NOT lifting the lid. Trust the process."
- "Use a caldero if you have one — the crispy bottom (pegao) is the best part."
- "Some families add pork chunks or olives. Make it your own."

#### Abuela's Tip
"The rice should be fluffy, not mushy. If it's sticky, you added too much water or lifted the lid."

## PAGE 5-6: RECIPE 2 — HABICHUELAS GUISTADAS

### Habichuelas Guisadas
*Stewed Beans — Puerto Rican Comfort Food*

**Prep:** 10 min | **Cook:** 30 min | **Serves:** 4-6

Every Puerto Rican grew up eating this. Creamy, savory, slightly sweet beans that go with everything — rice, meat, or just with bread.

#### Ingredients

- 2 cans (15 oz each) pink beans, drained
- 3 tablespoons sofrito
- 1 packet sazón con culantro y achiote
- 2 tablespoons tomato sauce
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 1 cup chicken broth
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon cumin
- Salt to taste
- 1 small potato, diced (optional — for thickness)

#### Instructions

1. **Heat oil** in a medium pot over medium heat.

2. **Add sofrito.** Sauté for 2-3 minutes.

3. **Add tomato sauce and tomato paste.** Stir and cook for 1 minute.

4. **Add beans, sazón, cumin, and sugar.** Stir well.

5. **Pour in chicken broth.** Add potato if using.

6. **Bring to a boil,** then reduce heat to medium-low.

7. **Simmer for 20-25 minutes,** stirring occasionally, until thick and creamy.

8. **Mash some beans** against the side of the pot for extra creaminess.

9. **Season with salt.** Serve over white rice.

#### Chef's Notes
- "The sugar isn't for sweetness — it balances the acidity of the tomatoes."
- "Some people add ham hock or salt pork for depth. We keep it simple."
- "These get better overnight. Make extra."

#### Abuela's Tip
"Let them simmer low and slow. Rushed beans taste rushed."

## PAGE 7: RECIPE 3 — TOSTONES

### Tostones
*Twice-Fried Green Plantains*

**Prep:** 5 min | **Cook:** 10 min | **Serves:** 4

The perfect side dish. Crispy, salty, addictive. Every Puerto Rican meal needs tostones.

#### Ingredients

- 2 green plantains
- Vegetable oil for frying
- Salt to taste
- Water (for the dip)

#### Instructions

1. **Peel plantains.** Cut off ends, score the skin lengthwise, peel under running water.

2. **Slice into 1-inch rounds.**

3. **Heat oil** (about 1 inch deep) in a skillet over medium-high heat.

4. **First fry:** Fry plantain rounds for 2-3 minutes per side until light golden. Remove and drain.

5. **Smash:** Place each round between two flat surfaces (cutting board, bottom of glass). Press down firmly to flatten to about 1/4 inch.

6. **Second fry:** Return smashed plantains to hot oil. Fry for 1-2 minutes per side until crispy and golden.

7. **Drain on paper towels.** Sprinkle with salt immediately.

8. **Serve hot** with ketchup, mayo-ketchup, or garlic sauce.

#### Chef's Notes
- "Plantains MUST be green. Yellow won't work — they'll be too sweet."
- "The double-fry is non-negotiable. First fry cooks, second fry crisps."
- "Serve immediately. They lose their crunch fast."

## PAGE 8: RECIPE 4 — FLAN DE QUESO

### Flan de Queso
*Puerto Rican Cheese Flan*

**Prep:** 20 min | **Cook:** 60 min | **Serves:** 8-10

The king of Puerto Rican desserts. Creamy, caramel-topped, and impossibly smooth. This is the recipe everyone asks for.

#### Ingredients

**For the caramel:**
- 1 cup sugar
- 1/4 cup water

**For the flan:**
- 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
- 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
- 1 package (8 oz) cream cheese, softened
- 5 eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/4 teaspoon salt

#### Instructions

1. **Preheat oven** to 350°F (175°C).

2. **Make caramel:** Heat sugar and water in a saucepan over medium heat. Swirl (don't stir) until golden amber. Immediately pour into a bundt pan or flan mold, tilting to coat the bottom and sides.

3. **Blend flan mixture:** In a blender, combine condensed milk, evaporated milk, cream cheese, eggs, vanilla, and salt. Blend until completely smooth.

4. **Pour over caramel** in the mold.

5. **Bake in water bath:** Place mold in a larger pan. Fill the larger pan with hot water until it reaches halfway up the mold.

6. **Bake for 55-60 minutes** until set but slightly jiggly in the center.

7. **Cool completely** at room temperature, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours (overnight is best).

8. **Unmold:** Run a knife around the edge. Place a plate on top and flip quickly. The caramel should flow down the sides.

#### Chef's Notes
- "The water bath is essential — it keeps the flan creamy, not rubbery."
- "Don't overbake. It should jiggle like jello when you take it out."
- "Make it the day before. It needs time to set."

#### Abuela's Tip
"Patience. The best flan is made with love and time."

## PAGE 9: RECIPE 5 — CAFÉ CON LECHE

### Café con Leche
*Puerto Rican Coffee with Milk*

**Prep:** 5 min | **Cook:** 5 min | **Serves:** 2

This isn't just coffee — it's a ritual. Strong, sweet, and essential to every Puerto Rican morning.

#### Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons Bustelo or Pilon coffee (ground)
- 1 cup water
- 1 cup whole milk (or evaporated milk for richness)
- 2-3 tablespoons sugar (adjust to taste)

#### Instructions

1. **Brew coffee** using a moka pot, cafetera, or drip method. Puerto Rican coffee is strong — use a fine grind.

2. **Heat milk** in a small saucepan until steaming (not boiling).

3. **Froth milk** by whisking vigorously or using a milk frother.

4. **Pour coffee** into cups, filling 1/2 to 2/3 full.

5. **Add sugar** to hot coffee and stir to dissolve.

6. **Top with frothed milk.** Serve immediately.

#### variations
- **Café con Leche Clásico:** Equal parts coffee and milk
- **Cortadito:** Less milk, more coffee — stronger
- **Café con Leche Helado:** Iced version for summer
- **Café Bombón:** Layered — condensed milk on bottom, coffee on top

#### Chef's Notes
- "Bustelo and Pilon are the brands. Don't substitute."
- "The moka pot is the traditional way. If you have one, use it."
- "Sugar goes in the coffee, not the milk."
