# PAGE 2: PUERTO RICAN BREAKFAST CULTURE

## La Cultura del Desayuno Boricua

Breakfast in Puerto Rico is more than a meal — it's a ritual, a connection to family, and a celebration of flavors that have been passed down through generations.

The traditional Puerto Rican breakfast features a combination of:

- **Panadería items** (bakery goods): Mallorcas, quesitos, mantecaditos
- **Hot beverages:** Café con leche, avena, chocolate caliente
- **Savory dishes:** Huevos con salchichas, tortilla de huevos
- **Grains:** Harina, magaz, farina

**The Café Corner:**
No Puerto Rican breakfast is complete without a hot cup of café. The cafetera (stovetop percolator) is a staple in every Puerto Rican kitchen. The ritual of making café — measuring the ground beans, pouring cold water, waiting for that first aromatic bubble — is as important as the coffee itself.

# PAGE 3-4: MALLORCAS

## Mallorcas / Mallorcas Puertorriqueñas

**Yield:** 12 rolls | **Prep Time:** 20 minutes | **Rise Time:** 2 hours | **Bake Time:** 18-20 minutes | **Total Time:** 2 hours 40 minutes

### Photo Description
Golden-brown spiral mallorcas arranged on a white ceramic plate, dusted generously with powdered sugar. A small glass cafecito cup sits to the right. The spiral pattern of the rolls is clearly visible, showing the layers of butter and cinnamon. Soft morning light illuminates the scene from a window on the left.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

**For the Dough:**
- 3 cups all-purpose flour (harina todo uso)
- ¼ cup sugar (azúcar)
- 1 packet (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast (levadura seca activa)
- ½ cup warm milk (leche tibia) — 110°F
- ½ cup unsalted butter, softened (mantequilla sin sal)
- 2 large eggs (huevos)
- 1 tsp salt (sal)
- 1 tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)

**For the Filling:**
- ½ cup unsalted butter, softened (mantequilla)
- ½ cup sugar (azúcar)
- 1 tbsp ground cinnamon (canela molida)
- 1 tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)

**For the Glaze:**
- 1 cup powdered sugar (azúcar en polvo)
- 2-3 tbsp milk (leche)
- ½ tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)

**For Topping:**
- ½ cup powdered sugar (azúcar en polvo) for dusting

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Activate the Yeast:**
In a small bowl, combine warm milk and sugar. Sprinkle yeast over the surface. Let stand for 5-10 minutes until foamy. If the yeast doesn't foam, it's expired — start with fresh yeast.

**Step 2 — Make the Dough:**
In a large mixing bowl, combine flour and salt. Make a well in the center. Add yeast mixture, softened butter, eggs, and vanilla. Stir with a wooden spoon until a shaggy dough forms. Turn out onto a floured surface.

**Step 3 — Knead:**
Knead the dough for 8-10 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should be soft but not sticky. Add flour 1 tablespoon at a time if needed.

**Step 4 — First Rise:**
Place dough in a greased bowl, cover with a damp kitchen towel or plastic wrap. Let rise in a warm place for 1-1½ hours until doubled in size.

**Step 5 — Prepare the Filling:**
While dough rises, mix softened butter, sugar, cinnamon, and vanilla in a small bowl until smooth. Set aside.

**Step 6 — Roll and Fill:**
Punch down dough. Roll into a rectangle approximately 18x12 inches on a floured surface. Spread filling evenly over the dough, leaving a ½-inch border. Starting from the long edge, tightly roll the dough into a log. Pinch the seam to seal.

**Step 7 — Cut and Shape:**
Using a sharp knife or dental floss (for clean cuts), cut the log into 12 equal slices. Place slices cut-side up in a greased 9x13-inch baking pan. Cover and let rise for 30-45 minutes until puffy.

**Step 8 — Bake:**
Preheat oven to 350°F (175°C). Bake for 18-20 minutes until golden brown on top.

**Step 9 — Glaze and Serve:**
While mallorcas cool slightly, whisk together powdered sugar, milk, and vanilla for the glaze. Drizzle over warm mallorcas. Dust generously with additional powdered sugar.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Make-Ahead Option:** After cutting and placing in the pan (Step 7), cover and refrigerate overnight. In the morning, let sit at room temperature for 30 minutes before baking.
- **Butter Tip:** Use room temperature butter for the filling — it spreads more easily and creates better layers.
- **Variation — Mallorcas de Coco:** Add ½ cup shredded coconut to the filling for a tropical twist.
- **Storage:** Best served fresh. Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Reheat in microwave for 15-20 seconds.
- **Troubleshooting:** If mallorcas are dense, your yeast may be expired. Always check the expiration date and test with the warm milk method first.

# PAGE 5-6: QUESITOS

## Quesitos / Quesitos de Queso Crema

**Yield:** 12 pastries | **Prep Time:** 25 minutes | **Bake Time:** 15-18 minutes | **Total Time:** 45 minutes

### Photo Description
A wire cooling rack holds golden-brown quesitos with visible cream cheese filling peeking through the puff pastry layers. The tops are glazed with a thin, shiny sugar coating. One quesito is broken in half, revealing the creamy white interior. A small bowl of guava paste sits in the background.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

**For the Pastry:**
- 2 sheets puff pastry (masa hojaldre), thawed
- 8 oz cream cheese, softened (queso crema)
- ¼ cup sugar (azúcar)
- 1 tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)
- 1 egg, beaten (huevo batido) for egg wash

**For the Glaze:**
- ½ cup powdered sugar (azúcar en polvo)
- 1-2 tbsp milk (leche)
- ¼ tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)

**Optional Fillings:**
- 4 tbsp guava paste (pasta de guayaba), cut into strips
- 4 tbsp pineapple jam (mermelada de piña)
- 4 tbsp dulce de leche

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Prepare the Cream Cheese Filling:**
In a medium bowl, beat cream cheese with sugar and vanilla until smooth and fluffy. If using a flavored filling, divide the cream cheese mixture and fold in guava, pineapple, or dulce de leche.

**Step 2 — Cut the Pastry:**
Roll out puff pastry sheets on a lightly floured surface. Cut each sheet into 6 equal rectangles (approximately 3x4 inches). You should have 12 rectangles total.

**Step 3 — Fill:**
Place 1 tablespoon of cream cheese filling in the center of each rectangle. If adding a flavored center, place the jam or dulce de leche on top of the cream cheese.

**Step 4 — Fold and Seal:**
Fold each rectangle in half lengthwise, creating a rectangle. Press the edges firmly with a fork to seal. Make sure the filling doesn't squeeze out.

**Step 5 — Egg Wash:**
Place quesitos on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Brush tops with beaten egg. Using a sharp knife, make 2-3 small slits on top of each quesito to allow steam to escape.

**Step 6 — Bake:**
Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C). Bake for 15-18 minutes until puffed and deep golden brown. The pastry should be crispy and flaky.

**Step 7 — Glaze:**
While quesitos cool for 5 minutes, whisk together powdered sugar, milk, and vanilla. Drizzle glaze over warm quesitos.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Puff Pastry Tip:** Keep puff pastry cold until ready to use. If it gets too soft, refrigerate for 10 minutes before proceeding.
- **Cream Cheese:** Use full-fat cream cheese for the best flavor and texture. Reduced-fat versions will be too soft.
- **Guava Tip:** If guava paste is too hard, microwave for 10-15 seconds to soften before cutting.
- **Serving:** Best served warm. Pair with café con leche for an authentic Puerto Rican breakfast.
- **Storage:** Store in airtight container at room temperature for 1 day, or refrigerate for up to 3 days. Reheat in oven at 300°F for 5 minutes.

# PAGE 7-8: MANTECADITOS

## Mantecaditos / Mantecaditos de Manteca

**Yield:** 24 cookies | **Prep Time:** 15 minutes | **Bake Time:** 12-15 minutes | **Total Time:** 30 minutes

### Photo Description
A rustic wooden cutting board displays a mound of buttery mantecaditos cookies, each with a characteristic cross-hatch pattern on top from fork pressing. The cookies are pale golden with slightly darker edges. A small glass jar of guava paste and a cup of coffee are visible in the soft-focus background.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

**For the Cookies:**
- 2 cups all-purpose flour (harina todo uso)
- ½ cup cornstarch (maicena)
- ½ cup powdered sugar (azúcar en polvo)
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened (mantequilla sin sal)
- 1 egg yolk (yema de huevo)
- 1 tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)
- ¼ tsp salt (sal)
- ½ tsp almond extract (extracto de almendra) — optional

**Optional Filling/Topping:**
- Guava paste (pasta de guayaba)
- Cream cheese (queso crema)
- Dulce de leche

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Preheat and Prepare:**
Preheat oven to 325°F (160°C). Line baking sheets with parchment paper.

**Step 2 — Cream the Butter:**
In a large bowl, beat softened butter with powdered sugar until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. Add egg yolk, vanilla, and almond extract (if using). Beat until combined.

**Step 3 — Add Dry Ingredients:**
In a separate bowl, whisk together flour, cornstarch, and salt. Gradually add to the butter mixture, mixing on low speed until just combined. Do not overmix.

**Step 4 — Shape:**
Roll dough into 1-inch balls. Place on prepared baking sheets, 2 inches apart. Using a fork, press down gently to flatten slightly, making a cross-hatch pattern on top.

**Step 5 — Bake:**
Bake for 12-15 minutes until edges are just barely golden. The centers should still be pale. Do not overbake — mantecaditos firm up as they cool.

**Step 6 — Cool:**
Let cool on baking sheet for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Texture:** The key to perfect mantecaditos is the cornstarch — it creates that melt-in-your-mouth texture that distinguishes them from regular butter cookies.
- **Shaping Tip:** If dough is too soft to roll, refrigerate for 15-20 minutes before shaping.
- **Filling Variation:** After baking, split cooled cookies and fill with guava paste or cream cheese.
- **Storage:** Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 1 week. They actually improve in flavor after a day.
- **Gift Idea:** Package in decorative tins with tissue paper for a beautiful homemade gift.

# PAGE 9-10: HUEVOS CON SALCHICHAS

## Huevos con Salchichas / Huevos con Salchichas

**Yield:** 4 servings | **Prep Time:** 10 minutes | **Cook Time:** 15 minutes | **Total Time:** 25 minutes

### Photo Description
A white oval plate holds a generous portion of scrambled eggs mixed with diced red hot dogs and sofrito, the eggs fluffy and yellow with specks of green cilantro and red pepper. A side of crispy tostones sits on the edge of the plate. A small dish of ketchup (traditional Puerto Rican accompaniment) is visible.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 8 large eggs (huevos grandes)
- 4 Puerto Rican-style hot dogs (salchichas), diced
- 2 tbsp sofrito (recado de sofrito)
- 1 tbsp olive oil (aceite de oliva)
- ¼ cup diced onion (cebolla picada)
- ¼ cup diced bell pepper (pimiento)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced (ajo)
- Salt and pepper to taste (sal y pimienta)
- 1 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped (cilantro fresco) for garnish
- Ketchup for serving (optional but traditional)

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Prep the Eggs:**
Crack eggs into a bowl. Add a pinch of salt and pepper. Beat with a fork until well combined but not frothy.

**Step 2 — Sauté the Hot Dogs:**
Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add diced hot dogs and cook for 3-4 minutes until slightly browned on edges.

**Step 3 — Add Aromatics:**
Add onion, bell pepper, and garlic. Cook for 2-3 minutes until vegetables soften. Add sofrito and cook for another minute until fragrant.

**Step 4 — Scramble the Eggs:**
Pour beaten eggs over the hot dog mixture. Let sit for 30 seconds, then gently stir with a spatula, pushing eggs from the edges to the center. Continue cooking for 3-4 minutes until eggs are set but still moist.

**Step 5 — Finish and Serve:**
Remove from heat (eggs will continue cooking). Garnish with fresh cilantro. Serve immediately with tostones and ketchup on the side.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Hot Dog Choice:** Use Puerto Rican-style hot dogs (like Badia brand) for authentic flavor. If unavailable, regular hot dogs work but will be milder.
- **Sofrito:** Homemade sofrito makes a huge difference. See our Sofrito & Sazón Master Class for the recipe.
- **Spice It Up:** Add a diced ají dulce or a splash of hot sauce for heat.
- **Serving:** Traditionally served with tostones, maduros, or white rice.
- **Make It a Meal:** Serve alongside Habichuelas Guisadas (stewed beans) for a complete Puerto Rican breakfast.

# PAGE 11-12: TORTILLA DE HUEVOS

## Tortilla de Huevos / Tortilla de Huevos

**Yield:** 4 servings | **Prep Time:** 10 minutes | **Cook Time:** 20 minutes | **Total Time:** 30 minutes

### Photo Description
A thick, golden-brown tortilla de huevos sits on a colorful Puerto Rican ceramic plate. The tortilla is about 1 inch thick with visible layers of potato and onion throughout. It's been cut into wedges, revealing the creamy egg interior. A simple green salad with vinaigrette sits beside it.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 8 large eggs (huevos)
- 2 medium potatoes, peeled and thinly sliced (papas)
- 1 medium onion, thinly sliced (cebolla)
- ½ cup olive oil (aceite de oliva) — for frying potatoes
- Salt and pepper to taste (sal y pimienta)
- 1 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped (perejil fresco) — optional

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Cook the Potatoes:**
Heat olive oil in a non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add sliced potatoes in a single layer (work in batches if needed). Cook for 8-10 minutes, turning occasionally, until golden and tender. Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels.

**Step 2 — Cook the Onions:**
In the same oil, add sliced onions. Cook for 3-4 minutes until softened and lightly caramelized. Remove and drain.

**Step 3 — Prepare the Eggs:**
In a large bowl, beat eggs with salt, pepper, and parsley (if using). Add the cooked potatoes and onions. Stir gently to combine.

**Step 4 — Cook the Tortilla:**
Heat 2 tablespoons of the potato-cooking oil in an 8-inch non-stick skillet over medium-low heat. Pour in the egg mixture. Cook for 5-6 minutes, gently shaking the pan occasionally to prevent sticking. The edges should set and the bottom should be golden.

**Step 5 — Flip the Tortilla:**
Place a large plate over the skillet. In one quick motion, flip the skillet so the tortilla lands on the plate. Slide the tortilla back into the skillet, cooked-side up. Cook for another 4-5 minutes until the bottom is golden and the center is set.

**Step 6 — Rest and Serve:**
Transfer to a serving plate. Let rest for 5 minutes before cutting into wedges. Serve warm or at room temperature.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Potato Tip:** Slice potatoes uniformly thin (about ⅛ inch) for even cooking. A mandoline helps.
- **Oil Use:** Don't skip the generous amount of olive oil — it's what gives the tortilla its characteristic flavor and prevents sticking.
- **Doneness Check:** Insert a knife into the center — if it comes out clean, the tortilla is done.
- **Room Temperature:** Tortilla de huevos is traditionally served at room temperature, making it perfect for meal prep.
- **Variations:** Add diced ham (jamón) or cooked chorizo for extra protein.

# PAGE 13-14: CAFÉ CON LECHE

## Café con Leche / Café con Leche Criollo

**Yield:** 2 cups | **Prep Time:** 5 minutes | **Cook Time:** 10 minutes | **Total Time:** 15 minutes

### Photo Description
Two traditional white ceramic cafecito cups sit on a small tray. The coffee is a rich, dark brown with a thin layer of crema on top. One cup has a spoon resting in it, showing the creamy texture. A small pitcher of steamed milk sits between the cups. Morning sunlight creates a warm glow.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

**For the Coffee:**
- ¼ cup finely ground dark roast coffee (café molido)
- 2 cups water (agua)
- 1 cinnamon stick (rola de canela) — optional
- 1 strip orange peel (cáscara de naranja) — optional

**For Serving:**
- 1 cup whole milk or evaporated milk (leche entera o evaporada)
- Sugar to taste (azúcar al gusto)
- Sweetened condensed milk (leche condensada) — optional, for a richer version

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Prepare the Cafetera:**
Fill the bottom chamber of your cafetera (stovetop percolator) with water. If using cinnamon stick or orange peel, add them to the water now.

**Step 2 — Add Coffee:**
Place the filter basket in the cafetera. Fill with ground coffee, pressing down gently but not too tightly. Screw on the top chamber securely.

**Step 3 — Brew:**
Place cafetera over medium heat. Brew for 8-10 minutes until you hear a gentle gurgling sound. Remove from heat immediately.

**Step 4 — Heat the Milk:**
While coffee brews, heat milk in a small saucepan over medium heat until steaming but not boiling. If using evaporated milk, it's already heated.

**Step 5 — Serve:**
Pour coffee into cups, filling halfway. Add hot milk to fill. Add sugar or sweetened condensed milk to taste. Stir gently.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Cafetera Care:** Your cafetera is a prized possession. Never put it in the dishwasher. Hand wash only.
- **Coffee Grind:** Use a fine grind for the cafetera. Pre-ground Puerto Rican coffee (like Café Yaucono or Alto Grande) is traditional.
- **Cinnamon Tip:** Add the cinnamon stick to the water before brewing — the heat extracts more flavor.
- **Condensed Milk Version:** For an ultra-creamy café con leche, use sweetened condensed milk instead of sugar and regular milk. This is called "café con leche condensada" and is beloved in Puerto Rico.
- **Iced Version:** Brew coffee stronger (use ⅓ cup grounds). Cool completely. Serve over ice with cold milk.

# PAGE 15-16: AVENA CALIENTE

## Avena Caliente / Avena Caliente con Canela

**Yield:** 4 servings | **Prep Time:** 5 minutes | **Cook Time:** 15 minutes | **Total Time:** 20 minutes

### Photo Description
A deep white bowl filled with creamy, thick avena (oatmeal) topped with a drizzle of condensed milk, a sprinkle of ground cinnamon, and a few raisins. The avena has a warm, golden-cream color. A cinnamon stick rests across the top of the bowl. Steam rises gently from the surface.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 1 cup rolled oats (avena en hojuelas)
- 3 cups water (agua)
- 1 cup milk (leche)
- ¼ cup sugar (azúcar)
- 1 cinnamon stick (rola de canela)
- ¼ tsp ground cinnamon (canela molida)
- ¼ tsp salt (sal)
- ½ tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)
- Sweetened condensed milk for drizzling (leche condensada) — optional
- Raisins for topping (pasas) — optional

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Boil Water:**
In a medium saucepan, bring water to a boil with the cinnamon stick. Let the cinnamon infuse for 2-3 minutes.

**Step 2 — Add Oats:**
Reduce heat to medium-low. Add oats, stirring constantly to prevent lumps. Cook for 5 minutes, stirring frequently.

**Step 3 — Add Milk and Seasonings:**
Add milk, sugar, salt, and ground cinnamon. Continue cooking for another 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick and creamy.

**Step 4 — Finish:**
Remove from heat. Stir in vanilla extract. Let stand for 2 minutes — it will thicken further.

**Step 5 — Serve:**
Ladle into bowls. Drizzle with sweetened condensed milk and sprinkle with extra cinnamon if desired. Add raisins if using.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Consistency:** Puerto Rican avena is thicker than American oatmeal. If too thick, add a splash more milk.
- **Quick Version:** Use quick-cooking oats and reduce cooking time to 8-10 minutes.
- **Overnight Option:** Make a double batch and reheat with a splash of milk for quick weekday breakfasts.
- **Variations:** Add shredded coconut, sliced almonds, or fresh fruit.
- **Kid-Friendly:** Reduce sugar and let kids add their own toppings.

# PAGE 17-18: CHOCOLATE CALIENTE

## Chocolate Caliente / Chocolate Caliente Boricua

**Yield:** 4 servings | **Prep Time:** 5 minutes | **Cook Time:** 15 minutes | **Total Time:** 20 minutes

### Photo Description
A tall glass mug filled with rich, dark chocolate caliente, topped with a generous dollop of whipped cream and a sprinkle of cocoa powder. The chocolate is thick and velvety. A traditional Puerto Rican wooden spoon (cucharita de madera) rests beside the mug. Dark chocolate pieces and cinnamon sticks are arranged nearby.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 4 tablets Mexican chocolate (chocolate de mesa) — about 3.5 oz total
- 4 cups whole milk (leche entera)
- 1 cup water (agua)
- 2 tbsp sugar (azúcar) — adjust to taste
- 1 cinnamon stick (rola de canela)
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon (canela molida) for garnish
- Whipped cream for topping (crema batida) — optional
- Marshmallows (malvaviscos) — optional

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Heat the Liquid:**
In a medium saucepan, combine milk and water. Add cinnamon stick. Heat over medium heat until steaming (do not boil).

**Step 2 — Add Chocolate:**
Break chocolate tablets into pieces. Add to the hot milk mixture. Whisk continuously until chocolate is completely melted and smooth, about 5-7 minutes.

**Step 3 — Sweeten:**
Add sugar and ground cinnamon. Whisk to combine. Taste and adjust sweetness.

**Step 4 — Froth (Optional):**
For a frothy texture, use a molinillo (traditional wooden whisk) or a regular whisk. Rub the molinillo between your palms rapidly to create foam.

**Step 5 — Serve:**
Remove cinnamon stick. Ladle into mugs. Top with whipped cream, extra cinnamon, or marshmallows if desired.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Chocolate Choice:** Abuelita or Ibarra brand Mexican chocolate are traditional. If unavailable, use 3 oz dark chocolate + 2 tbsp sugar + ½ tsp cinnamon.
- **Thickness:** Puerto Rican chocolate caliente is thicker than American hot chocolate. For an even thicker version, add 1 tbsp cornstarch dissolved in 2 tbsp cold milk.
- **Molinillo:** This traditional wooden whisk is available at Latin grocery stores. It creates the perfect froth.
- **Adult Version:** Add a splash of rum or Kahlúa for a grown-up treat.
- **Cool Weather Comfort:** Perfect for rainy days. Serve with mallorcas for dipping.

# PAGE 19-20: MAGAZ DE MAÍZ

## Magaz de Maíz / Magaz de Maíz

**Yield:** 6 servings | **Prep Time:** 10 minutes | **Cook Time:** 45 minutes | **Total Time:** 55 minutes

### Photo Description
A creamy, pale yellow magaz served in individual ramekins, each topped with a pool of sweetened condensed milk and a sprinkle of ground cinnamon. The texture is smooth and pudding-like. The ramekins sit on a colorful Puerto Rican hand-painted tray. A cinnamon stick and corn husk are used as garnish.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 2 cups fresh corn kernels (maíz fresco) — or 1 (15 oz) can whole kernel corn, drained
- 2 cups whole milk (leche entera)
- ½ cup sugar (azúcar)
- 2 tbsp cornstarch (maicena)
- 2 eggs, beaten (huevos batidos)
- 1 tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)
- ¼ tsp ground cinnamon (canela molida)
- Pinch of salt (pizca de sal)
- Sweetened condensed milk for topping (leche condensada)
- Ground cinnamon for garnish (canela molida)

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Blend the Corn:**
If using fresh corn, cut kernels from cob. Place corn kernels and milk in a blender. Blend until mostly smooth (some texture is okay). If using canned corn, blend with milk until smooth.

**Step 2 — Strain (Optional):**
For a smoother texture, strain the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve, pressing with a spoon to extract all liquid. Discard solids.

**Step 3 — Cook:**
Pour corn-milk mixture into a medium saucepan. Add sugar, cinnamon, and salt. Cook over medium heat, stirring frequently, for 15-20 minutes until mixture thickens.

**Step 4 — Temper the Eggs:**
In a small bowl, slowly pour ½ cup of the hot corn mixture into the beaten eggs, whisking constantly. This prevents the eggs from scrambling.

**Step 5 — Add Egg Mixture:**
Pour the tempered egg mixture back into the saucepan. Cook for another 5-7 minutes, stirring constantly, until thick and pudding-like.

**Step 6 — Add Vanilla:**
Remove from heat. Stir in vanilla extract.

**Step 7 — Serve:**
Pour into individual ramekins or a serving dish. Let cool to room temperature, then refrigerate for at least 2 hours. Serve cold, topped with sweetened condensed milk and a sprinkle of cinnamon.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Fresh vs. Canned:** Fresh corn gives the best flavor, but canned works well in a pinch.
- **Consistency:** Magaz should be thick enough to hold its shape when scooped with a spoon.
- **Make-Ahead:** Magaz can be made up to 3 days ahead and stored in the refrigerator.
- **Serving Temperature:** Traditionally served cold, but can be enjoyed warm.
- **Variation:** Add ½ cup shredded coconut to the mixture for Coconut Magaz.

# PAGE 21: HARINA CON LECHE

## Harina con Leche / Harina con Leche

**Yield:** 4 servings | **Prep Time:** 5 minutes | **Cook Time:** 10 minutes | **Total Time:** 15 minutes

### Photo Description
A warm bowl of harina con leche, creamy and smooth with a light tan color. The surface has a slight sheen. A drizzle of condensed milk creates a swirl pattern on top, and a dusting of ground cinnamon adds warmth. A spoon rests in the bowl, showing the thick, porridge-like consistency.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 1 cup farina or cream of wheat (harina de trigo)
- 3 cups water (agua)
- 1 cup milk (leche)
- ¼ cup sugar (azúcar)
- ¼ tsp salt (sal)
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon (canela molida)
- ½ tsp vanilla extract (vainilla)
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter (mantequilla sin sal)
- Sweetened condensed milk for drizzling (leche condensada) — optional

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Boil Water:**
In a medium saucepan, bring water to a boil with salt and cinnamon.

**Step 2 — Add Farina:**
Reduce heat to medium-low. Slowly pour farina into the boiling water in a thin stream, whisking constantly to prevent lumps.

**Step 3 — Cook:**
Cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring frequently, until thick and smooth.

**Step 4 — Add Milk and Butter:**
Stir in milk, sugar, and butter. Continue cooking for another 2-3 minutes until heated through and creamy.

**Step 5 — Finish:**
Remove from heat. Stir in vanilla extract.

**Step 6 — Serve:**
Pour into bowls. Drizzle with sweetened condensed milk and dust with extra cinnamon if desired.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Lump Prevention:** The key is to pour the farina slowly while whisking vigorously. Never dump it all in at once.
- **Consistency Control:** Add more water or milk if the harina becomes too thick. It will continue to thicken as it cools.
- **Quick Breakfast:** This is one of the fastest Puerto Rican breakfasts — ready in under 15 minutes.
- **Kid-Favorite:** A comforting, warm breakfast that children love. Add extra sugar for little ones.
- **Variation:** Top with fresh fruit, nuts, or a spoonful of guava paste.

# PAGE 22: PAN CON MANTEQUILLA

## Pan con Mantequilla / Pan con Mantequilla y Café

**Yield:** 4 servings | **Prep Time:** 5 minutes | **Cook Time:** 0 minutes | **Total Time:** 5 minutes

### Photo Description
A simple but beautiful arrangement: sliced Puerto Rican pan de water bread on a wooden board, each slice generously spread with soft butter. A cafecito cup sits beside the bread. The bread has a golden crust and soft, white interior. This is the quintessential Puerto Rican breakfast — simple, comforting, and perfect.

### Ingredientes / Ingredients

- 1 loaf Puerto Rican water bread (pan de agua) — or French bread
- ½ cup unsalted butter, softened (mantequilla sin sal)
- Café con leche for serving (see recipe on page 13)
- Optional spreads: guava paste, cream cheese, jam

### Instrucciones / Instructions

**Step 1 — Slice the Bread:**
Slice the bread into ½-inch thick slices. For the most authentic experience, use a serrated knife.

**Step 2 — Prepare the Butter:**
Ensure butter is at room temperature for easy spreading. For extra flavor, beat the butter with a pinch of salt until fluffy.

**Step 3 — Toast (Optional):**
While traditional pan con mantequilla uses fresh bread, lightly toasting adds a nice crunch. Toast under the broiler for 1-2 minutes.

**Step 4 — Spread and Serve:**
Generously spread each slice with butter. Serve immediately with a hot cup of café con leche.

### Chef's Notes / Notas del Chef

- **Bread Choice:** Pan de agua is the traditional Puerto Rican bread for this dish. If unavailable, French bread or Italian bread works.
- **Butter Tip:** For a Puerto Rican twist, mix softened butter with a pinch of garlic powder and parsley for a savory version.
- **Traditional Experience:** This is how many Puerto Ricans start their day — simple bread and butter with café con leche. It's not fancy, but it's home.
- **Pairings:** Serve with huevos con salchichas or tortilla de huevos for a more substantial breakfast.
