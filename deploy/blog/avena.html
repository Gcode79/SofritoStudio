Avena

Creamy, cinnamon-spiced Puerto Rican oatmeal drink — warm or cold, ready in 10 minutes.

10 minutes Easy

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·4 min read

Avena is the Puerto Rican oatmeal drink — creamy, cinnamon-spiced, and served warm or cold. It's the breakfast smoothie before smoothies existed, a morning staple across the island. Long before protein shakes and oat milk lattes became trendy, boricuas were blending oats with water, sugar, and cinnamon into a drink that could fuel a whole morning. You'll find avena in thermoses at breakfast tables, in plastic cups from roadside vendors, and in cafeterias next to the café con leche. It's comforting, filling, and costs almost nothing to make. This is the drink your abuela made when she wanted everyone to have something warm and nourishing before heading out the door.

What is Avena?

Avena is a traditional Puerto Rican oatmeal drink made from blended rolled oats, water, sugar, and cinnamon. Unlike American oatmeal, which is eaten with a spoon, avena is a smooth, pourable drink — think of it as the island's original smoothie. The oats are blended until silky, strained to remove any grittiness, and then simmered with sweetened water until the drink thickens just enough to coat the back of a spoon.

It's served both warm in the morning and cold as an afternoon refreshment. Some families add a splash of milk, others stick to the traditional water base. The beauty of avena is its simplicity — four or five ingredients, one blender, one pot, and ten minutes. That's it. No fancy equipment, no exotic ingredients, just a drink that has nourished Puerto Rican families for generations.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
4 servings

Serves 4:

  • 1 cup rolled oats — old-fashioned oats work best. Quick oats dissolve too much and lose body.
  • 4 cups water — split into 2 cups for blending and 2 cups for the pot.
  • 1/2 cup sugar — adjust to taste. Some families use less; others add a bit more.
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract — adds warmth and depth beyond the cinnamon.
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon — the signature spice. Use more for dusting on top.
  • Pinch of salt — brightens all the flavors.

Mainland Swaps

  • No rolled oats? Quick oats work but produce a thinner drink. Steel-cut oats give a grittier texture — not recommended unless you strain very well.
  • Want it creamier? Substitute 1 cup of the water with whole milk or coconut milk for a richer version.
  • Less sugar? Use 1/4 cup sugar or swap for honey or condensed milk. Adjust to your preference.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Blend the oats. Add the rolled oats and 2 cups of water to a blender. Blend on high until the mixture is completely smooth and creamy — about 60 seconds. No visible oat pieces should remain.
2
Heat the sweetened water. In a medium pot, combine the remaining 2 cups of water, sugar, and cinnamon. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
3
Strain and combine. Pour the blended oat mixture through a fine-mesh strainer into the hot water. Press with a spoon to extract all the liquid. Discard the solids.
4
Add vanilla and salt. Stir in the vanilla extract and pinch of salt. These last two ingredients make a bigger difference than you'd expect.
5
Simmer 3 minutes. Cook over medium-low heat for 3 minutes, stirring constantly to prevent sticking. The avena will thicken slightly — it should coat the back of a spoon but still pour easily.
6
Serve. Pour into mugs for a warm morning drink, or refrigerate for 2 hours and serve cold. Dust with extra cinnamon if you like.

Tips for Perfect Avena

  • Blend well. The smoother the oats blend, the creamier your avena. Blend for a full minute — don't rush this step.
  • Strain thoroughly. Use a fine-mesh strainer or cheesecloth. Unstrained avena has a gritty texture that most people don't enjoy.
  • Stir while simmering. Oat mixtures love to stick and clump. Constant stirring for those final 3 minutes makes the difference between silky and lumpy.
  • Serve it your way. Warm avena is a morning comfort drink. Cold avena is an afternoon refresher. Both are equally authentic — there's no wrong way to drink it.
