Coquito

Puerto Rico's coconut eggnog — the holiday drink that replaces everything from November through January.

15 min + chill Easy

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·5 min read

There's a moment every November in every Puerto Rican household when the first bottle of coquito appears — and suddenly the holidays have officially started. Coquito is Puerto Rico's answer to eggnog, but richer, creamier, and built on coconut milk instead of eggs. It's a sweet, spiced, rum-laced drink that gets poured over ice and sipped while the pernil roasts, while the Christmas lights go up, while the familia gathers in the kitchen. Every family has a secret recipe. Some add egg yolks. Some spike it with brandy instead of rum. Some swear by a specific brand of coconut milk. And every family is absolutely convinced theirs is the best. Coquito isn't just a drink — it's a holiday tradition wrapped in a blender, chilled in the fridge, and shared from house to house in recycled bottles that get passed around like gifts.

What is Coquito?

Coquito is a Puerto Rican coconut-based holiday drink — essentially coconut eggnog without the eggs (though some families do add them). The base is coconut milk, sweetened condensed milk, and evaporated milk, blended with white rum, vanilla, cinnamon, and nutmeg until smooth and frothy. It's served ice cold, often in small glasses because it's rich enough that a little goes a long way. The drink emerged from Puerto Rico's holiday traditions and has become a staple across the diaspora — from New York to Orlando to Chicago, every boricua household has a coquito recipe they guard closely.

Unlike Mexican rompope or American eggnog, coquito doesn't rely on eggs for its creaminess. The coconut milk and condensed milk do all the work, creating a thick, velvety base that's naturally dairy-free in its simplest form (though the evaporated milk technically makes it not vegan). The rum is essential — it cuts through the sweetness and adds warmth. Some families use aguardiente, some use brandy, and some use both. But the classic version is white rum, and that's what we're making here.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
8 servings

Makes about 8 servings:

  • 2 cans (13.5 oz) coconut milk — full fat, not light. The fat is what makes coquito creamy. Thai or Goya brands both work well.
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk — this is the sweetener and thickener in one. Don't substitute with evaporated milk.
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk — adds body and a slightly caramelized dairy note that rounds out the coconut.
  • 1 cup white rum — Puerto Rican rum is traditional (Bacardí, Don Q, or Ron del Barrilito). Use a good-quality mixing rum, not bottom shelf.
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract — real vanilla, not imitation. It adds warmth and depth.
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon — for blending in. You'll also dust more on top when serving.
  • Pinch of ground nutmeg — fresh-grated is best if you have a microplane. A little goes a long way.

Variations

  • Virgin coquito (no rum): Simply omit the rum. Add an extra teaspoon of vanilla and a splash of coconut extract to compensate for the missing depth. Kids love it.
  • Chocolate coquito: Add 2 tablespoons of cocoa powder or chocolate syrup to the blender. It's become increasingly popular and is delicious.
  • Coffee coquito: Dissolve 2 tablespoons of instant espresso in a splash of warm water before adding to the blender. Perfect for the coffee lovers in the family.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Add everything to the blender. Pour in the coconut milk, sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, rum, vanilla, cinnamon, and nutmeg. Don't worry about the order — it all goes in at once.
2
Blend until smooth. Blend on high for 1-2 minutes. The mixture should be completely smooth, frothy on top, and uniform in color. If your blender is small, do it in two batches.
3
Taste and adjust. This is where your personal touch comes in. Need it sweeter? Add a splash more condensed milk. Want more warmth? Add another pinch of cinnamon. More kick? More rum. Make it yours.
4
Chill it. Pour into glass bottles or mason jars, seal tightly, and refrigerate for at least 4 hours — overnight is better. The coconut milk thickens as it chills and the flavors meld together. Don't skip this step.
5
Serve and enjoy. Pour over ice in small glasses (it's rich — 4-6 oz is plenty). Dust the top with ground cinnamon. Close your eyes and you're in your abuela's kitchen on Nochebuena.

Tips for Perfect Coquito

  • Use full-fat coconut milk. Light coconut milk makes a thin, watery coquito. The fat is what gives it that creamy, velvety texture. Shake the can well before opening.
  • Chill overnight. Four hours is the minimum, but overnight is dramatically better. The drink thickens as it chills, the spices bloom, and the rum mellows into the coconut base.
  • Shake before serving. Coquito can separate in the fridge. Give the bottle a vigorous shake before pouring — it comes right back together.
  • Give it as gifts. Pour into decorative bottles, add a ribbon, and give to neighbors and friends. It's the Puerto Rican equivalent of holiday cookies — but better.
