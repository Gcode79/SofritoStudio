Flan de Queso

The creamiest Puerto Rican cheesecake flan — rich, silky, and topped with a perfect caramel that pools on the plate.

⏱ 80 min 🍽 10 servings 📖 Intermediate
By Josh Ortiz · August 17, 2026 · 10 min read

If there's one dessert that screams Puerto Rican celebration, it's flan. And not just any flan — flan de queso, the cheesecake version that's richer, creamier, and more decadent than the classic. This is the one my abuela made for every birthday, holiday, and "just because" Sunday.

What Makes Flan de Queso Different

Traditional flan is a crème caramel — eggs, milk, sugar, vanilla. Flan de queso adds cream cheese to the custard base, which gives it a density and tang that sits somewhere between flan and New York cheesecake. The texture is silkier, richer, and holds its shape better when sliced. Once you try it, regular flan feels like a sidegrade.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
10 servings
  • 1 granulated sugar (for the caramel)
  • 5 eggs
  • 1 (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 8 cream cheese, softened to room temperature
  • 1 vanilla extract
  • 1/4 salt

Step-by-Step Instructions

Step 1 — Prep the water bath. Preheat your oven to 350°F. Place a large roasting pan on the center rack and fill it with about 1 inch of hot water. The water bath (baño María) keeps the heat gentle and even — skip it and you'll get bubbles and a rubbery texture.

Step 2 — Make the caramel. Add the sugar to a heavy-bottomed saucepan over medium heat. Stir with a wooden spoon as it melts and clumps. Once it turns to liquid, stop stirring and swirl the pan until it's a deep amber color — like honey. Work fast: caramel goes from perfect to burned in seconds. Pour it immediately into a 9-inch round baking dish and swirl to coat the bottom and sides.

Step 3 — Blend the custard. In a blender, combine the eggs, condensed milk, evaporated milk, softened cream cheese, vanilla, and salt. Blend on high for 2 full minutes until completely smooth. Scrape down the sides and blend again — any cream cheese lumps will show up in the finished flan.

Step 4 — Strain it. Pour the custard through a fine-mesh strainer into the caramel-lined dish. This catches any unblended bits, chalazae from the eggs, and air bubbles. It's an extra step, but it's the difference between a silky flan and a slightly grainy one.

Step 5 — Bake in the water bath. Carefully place the flan dish into the roasting pan with hot water. The water should come about halfway up the sides of the flan dish. Bake for 55-65 minutes. The flan is done when the edges are set but the center still has a slight jiggle — like Jell-O, not like liquid.

Step 6 — Chill and flip. Let the flan cool on a wire rack to room temperature, then cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours — overnight is better. To serve, run a thin knife around the edge, place a large plate on top, and flip it in one confident motion. The caramel should pool beautifully around the flan.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold cream cheese. If it's not fully softened, you'll get lumps no amount of blending can fix. Leave it out for an hour.
  • Over-baking. A fully set, firm flan is an overbaked flan. Pull it when the center jiggles — it continues to set as it cools.
  • Rushing the chill time. Four hours minimum. Overnight is best. Warm flan doesn't unmold cleanly and the caramel hasn't developed its sauce yet.
  • Skipping the water bath. The oven's direct heat will curdle the custard and create bubbles. The water bath is non-negotiable.

Serving & Storing

Flan de queso serves 10 slices easily — it's rich, so a little goes a long way. Serve it cold straight from the fridge. It keeps beautifully for up to 5 days covered in the refrigerator. The caramel will continue to thin out and become more saucy over time, which is honestly a bonus.

For holidays, I often make two — one for the party and one to send home with family. It's the kind of dessert that people ask about for weeks after.
