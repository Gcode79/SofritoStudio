Habichuelas Guisadas

Thick, savory stewed beans with calabaza — the everyday Puerto Rican side that goes with every plate of rice.

35 minutes Easy

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·6 min read

If there's one side dish that belongs on every Puerto Rican plate, it's habichuelas guisadas — thick, savory stewed beans that simmer low and slow until the broth turns velvety and the pumpkin melts into the sauce. While arroz con gandules gets the headlines, habichuelas guisadas is the quiet workhorse of the cocina, the dish that makes a simple plate of white rice feel like a meal. Every boricua grew up with a pot of these beans simmering on the stove, the smell of sofrito and tomato filling the house. The secret weapon is calabaza — Caribbean pumpkin that naturally thickens the stew as it softens and partially breaks down. Whether you're serving it alongside rice and pernil or spooning it over pasta (yes, that's a thing), habichuelas guisadas is comfort in a bowl.

What Are Habichuelas Guisadas?

Habichuelas guisadas translates simply to "stewed beans" — but that undersells what's happening in the pot. Pink beans (or sometimes red beans) are simmered in a base of sofrito, tomato paste, and sazon, with chunks of calabaza that dissolve into the broth to create a thick, almost creamy consistency without any cream. Unlike refried beans or Cuban-style black beans, Puerto Rican habichuelas guisadas are chunky and saucy — thick enough to coat a spoon but loose enough to pour over rice. The calabaza is the key ingredient that sets this apart from every other bean stew in the Caribbean. As it simmers, it softens and partially breaks down, naturally thickening the broth and adding a subtle sweetness that balances the savory tomato and sofrito base.

You'll find habichuelas guisadas at virtually every Puerto Rican meal — from weekday lunches to holiday feasts. It's the side that never gets skipped, the dish that kids dip their tostones into, and the leftovers that taste even better the next day stirred into a bowl of rice. Some families make it with kidney beans, some with pink beans, and some with pigeon peas, but the method stays the same: sofrito, tomato, calabaza, patience.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
6 servings

For 6 servings:

  • 2 cans (15 oz) pink beans — do NOT drain. The liquid is what builds the stew's body. Goya pink beans are the standard.
  • 1/4 cup sofrito — homemade or store-bought. This is the aromatic backbone of the whole dish.
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste — adds depth and color. Cook it briefly to caramelize and remove the raw taste.
  • 1/2 cup pumpkin (calabaza) cubed — Caribbean pumpkin, about 1-inch pieces. Find it frozen at Latin groceries or substitute butternut squash.
  • 1 packet sazon con culantro y achiote — for color and that signature Puerto Rican flavor.
  • 1 teaspoon cumin — warm and earthy, it pairs perfectly with the beans.
  • 1 cup water — to thin the stew to the right consistency. Use chicken broth for extra richness.
  • 2 tablespoons oil — vegetable or olive oil for sauteing the sofrito.
  • Salt to taste

Mainland Swaps

  • No calabaza? Butternut squash is the closest substitute — same sweetness and soft texture when cooked. Sugar pumpkin works too.
  • No pink beans? Red kidney beans are the most common alternative. Pinto beans work in a pinch, though the flavor is milder.
  • No sofrito? Mince 3 garlic cloves + 1/4 cup onion + 1/4 cup green pepper + a few cilantro stems. It won't be the same, but it'll get you close.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Saute the sofrito. Heat oil in a medium pot over medium heat. Add the sofrito and cook for 3 minutes, stirring often, until it softens and fills the kitchen with that unmistakable aroma.
2
Add tomato paste. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 1 minute, letting it caramelize slightly. This step deepens the flavor and gets rid of any tinny taste.
3
Add pumpkin and spices. Toss in the cubed calabaza, sazon packet, and cumin. Stir until the pumpkin is coated in the flavor base. The achiote in the sazon will start turning everything that beautiful orange-red.
4
Add beans and water. Pour in both cans of pink beans — liquid and all — plus the cup of water. Stir everything together and bring to a boil.
5
Simmer 20 minutes. Reduce heat to medium-low and let it bubble gently. The pumpkin will soften, the broth will thicken, and the flavors will meld. Stir occasionally so nothing sticks.
6
Mash and serve. Use the back of your spoon to mash some of the pumpkin pieces against the side of the pot. This is the trick that makes the broth thick and creamy. Taste and adjust salt — serve hot over white rice.

Tips for Perfect Habichuelas Guisadas

  • Don't drain the beans. The can liquid is starchy and flavorful — it's what gives the stew its body. Drain them and you'll need to add more thickener.
  • Mash some pumpkin. This is the non-negotiable step. Press half the pumpkin pieces against the pot to dissolve them into the broth. Leave the rest whole for texture.
  • Low and slow wins. A rapid boil breaks the beans apart and makes the stew grainy. A gentle simmer keeps the beans whole while the pumpkin melts.
  • It thickens as it cools. If the stew seems a little thin when you take it off the heat, give it 5 minutes. It'll tighten up. Add a splash of water when reheating leftovers.
