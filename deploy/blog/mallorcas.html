Mallorcas

Puerto Rican sweet bread rolls — buttery, slightly dense, and perfect for breakfast dipped in café con leche.

2 hours Medium

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·7 min read

The smell of mallorcas fresh from the oven is the smell of a Puerto Rican morning. These sweet, buttery bread rolls are a fixture of every panadería (bakery) on the island and a breakfast staple in boricua homes across the diaspora. They're slightly denser than a dinner roll, with a tender crumb and a subtle sweetness that makes them perfect for tearing open and dunking into a hot cup of café con leche. The name comes from the Spanish island of Mallorca (Majorca), reflecting the bread's Mediterranean roots — but Puerto Ricans made it entirely their own. Every panadería has its own version: some spiral the dough, some stuff them with guava or cream cheese, and some keep it classic with a mountain of powdered sugar on top. This recipe gives you the classic — a soft, buttery roll that comes together with simple ingredients and a little patience with the yeast.

What Are Mallorcas?

Mallorcas are Puerto Rican sweet bread rolls — enriched dough rolls made with butter, eggs, milk, and sugar, then baked until golden and dusted with powdered sugar. They're a type of pan dulce (sweet bread) that has become one of the most recognizable items in Puerto Rican baking. Unlike American sweet rolls (which tend to be light and fluffy), mallorcas have a slightly denser, more bread-like texture — somewhere between a brioche and a dinner roll. They're not as sweet as a cinnamon roll, not as rich as a croissant, but occupy that perfect middle ground that makes them ideal for breakfast.

Walk into any Puerto Rican panadería early in the morning and you'll see them piled high in glass cases — golden-brown spirals dusted with white powdered sugar, sometimes filled with guava paste or cream cheese. They're eaten warm, torn by hand, dipped into coffee, and argued about (every family has a preferred panadería). Making them at home brings that bakery experience into your kitchen, and while the rising time means planning ahead, the actual hands-on work is minimal.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
12 servings

Makes 12 rolls:

  • 3 all-purpose flour — spooned and leveled for accuracy. Bread flour works too for a chewier texture.
  • 1/4 sugar — just enough for subtle sweetness. These aren't meant to be dessert-sweet.
  • 1 (2 1/4 tsp) active dry yeast — check the expiration date. Old yeast won't rise.
  • 1/2 warm milk — heated to 110°F (warm to the touch, not hot). Whole milk gives the richest result.
  • 1/3 butter, softened — unsalted, room temperature. This is what makes mallorcas tender and rich.
  • 2 eggs — at room temperature for better incorporation into the dough.
  • 1 vanilla extract — adds warmth and a subtle fragrance to the bread.
  • 1/2 salt — balances the sweetness and strengthens the gluten.
  • Powdered sugar for dusting — generous. Use a fine-mesh sieve for an even coating.

Variations

  • Guava-filled: Flatten each dough ball, place a spoonful of guava paste in the center, and fold closed before placing seam-side down in the pan.
  • Cream cheese-filled: Same technique — a tablespoon of sweetened cream cheese in the center of each roll before rising.
  • Cinnamon sugar: Roll the dough out flat, spread with butter, sprinkle cinnamon sugar, roll up like a log, and cut into 12 spirals. Place cut-side up in the pan.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Bloom the yeast. Dissolve the yeast in warm milk (110°F) with a pinch of sugar. Let it sit 5-10 minutes until foamy and bubbly. If it doesn't foam, your yeast is dead — start over with fresh yeast.
2
Mix the dough. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, and salt. Pour in the yeast mixture, add the softened butter, eggs, and vanilla. Mix until a shaggy dough forms — it'll look messy. That's normal.
3
Knead for 10 minutes. Turn the dough onto a floured surface and knead until smooth and elastic. It should spring back slowly when you poke it. The butter will make it feel slippery at first — keep going. It comes together.
4
First rise. Place the dough in a greased bowl, cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap, and let it rise in a warm spot for 1 hour — until doubled in size. A turned-off oven with the light on is a great proofing box.
5
Shape and second rise. Punch the dough down gently and divide into 12 equal pieces. Roll each into a smooth ball and place in a greased 9x13 pan (3 rows of 4). Cover and let rise another 30 minutes until puffy.
6
Bake and dust. Bake at 375°F for 18-20 minutes until golden on top. While they're still warm, dust generously with powdered sugar through a fine-mesh sieve. Serve warm with café con leche for the full experience.

Tips for Perfect Mallorcas

  • Check your yeast. If the milk and yeast mixture doesn't foam after 10 minutes, the yeast is dead. Start over with fresh yeast and make sure the milk is warm (not hot) — anything above 120°F kills yeast.
  • Don't rush the rise. The rising time is what develops the flavor and texture. A cold rise overnight in the fridge (covered tightly) actually improves the flavor — just bring to room temperature before shaping.
  • Room temperature ingredients. Cold butter and eggs don't incorporate well into the dough. Take them out 30-60 minutes before you start.
  • Dust while warm. The powdered sugar sticks best when the rolls are still warm from the oven. If they cool completely, the sugar slides off. Serve within 30 minutes of baking for the best experience.
