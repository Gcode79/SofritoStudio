Pinchos

Puerto Rico's grilled skewers — the beach food, the party food, the "I don't feel like cooking" food.

30 min + marinade Easy

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·7 min read

Pinchos are Puerto Rican grilled skewers — the beach food, the party food, the "I don't feel like cooking" food. Every kiosk and roadside vendor has their own marinade recipe. They're the skewers you smell wafting from every fritura on the island — chunks of juicy chicken or pork, threaded onto sticks, grilled until charred, and finished with a brush of garlic butter. In Puerto Rico, pinchos are synonymous with gathering. They're at every backyard barbecue, every beach day, every festival. The marinade is simple but potent: olive oil, garlic, oregano, vinegar, and sazon. The meat absorbs all of it, then the grill adds that smoky char that makes them irresistible. Whether you're using a charcoal grill or your oven's broiler, pinchos deliver that unmistakable boricua street food experience.

What are Pinchos?

Pinchos (sometimes spelled "pinchos") are Puerto Rican meat skewers, similar in concept to kabobs but distinctly boricua in flavor. The word comes from the Spanish "pinchar" — to pierce or stab. They typically feature chicken thighs or pork loin cut into chunks, marinated in a garlicky, herbaceous mixture, and threaded onto wooden skewers. The marinade is the star: olive oil, minced garlic, dried oregano, vinegar, and a packet of sazon give the meat a deeply savory, slightly tangy, beautifully colored profile. After grilling or broiling, they get a final brush of garlic butter — because in Puerto Rico, butter makes everything better. Pinchos are served as a main dish, a snack, or a party appetizer. They pair perfectly with tostones, rice, or just on their own.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
4 servings

Serves 4:

  • 1.5 lbs chicken thighs or pork — boneless, skinless. Thighs stay juicier than breast. Pork loin works great too.
  • 1/4 cup olive oil — the base of the marinade. Helps distribute the flavors and prevents sticking.
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced — fresh garlic is non-negotiable. The more the better.
  • 1 tbsp dried oregano — the earthy, slightly bitter backbone of the marinade.
  • 1 tbsp vinegar — white vinegar or apple cider vinegar. Tenderizes the meat and adds brightness.
  • 1 packet sazon con culantro y achiote — for that signature color and flavor.
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Wooden skewers, soaked in water for 30 minutes — prevents burning on the grill.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Cut and marinate the meat. Cut the chicken or pork into 1.5-inch chunks. Place in a large bowl and add the olive oil, minced garlic, oregano, vinegar, sazon, salt, and pepper. Toss until every piece is coated. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour — overnight is even better.
2
Thread onto skewers. Remove the meat from the marinade and thread onto soaked wooden skewers. Leave a small gap between pieces so heat circulates evenly. Don't pack them too tight — air flow is what gives you that char.
3
Grill or broil. Heat your grill to medium-high (or set your oven broiler to high). Cook the pinchos for 4-5 minutes per side until charred on the edges and cooked through. The internal temperature should reach 165°F for chicken or 145°F for pork.
4
Brush with garlic butter. As soon as the pinchos come off the heat, brush them generously with melted garlic butter. The butter melts into the charred crevices and adds a rich finish that elevates the whole skewer. Serve immediately.

Tips for Perfect Pinchos

  • Marinate longer. One hour is the minimum, but 4 hours or overnight makes a massive difference. The vinegar and salt work to tenderize the meat while the garlic and oregano permeate every fiber.
  • Use chicken thighs. They have more fat than breast meat, which means more flavor and more moisture. They're also more forgiving on the grill — harder to overcook.
  • Soak your skewers. Wooden skewers will burn and break if they go on the grill dry. Soak them in water for at least 30 minutes before threading.
  • Don't skip the garlic butter. It's the finishing touch that separates good pinchos from great pinchos. Melt butter with a crushed garlic clove and brush it on while the meat is still sizzling hot.
