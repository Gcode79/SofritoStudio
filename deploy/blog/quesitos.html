Quesitos

Cream cheese-filled puff pastries — golden, flaky, and gone in one bite.

30 minutes Easy

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·5 min read

Quesitos are cream cheese-filled puff pastries — the bakery staple that's gone in one bite. Every panadería in Puerto Rico has a tray of these golden, sugar-dusted pastries, and they're usually the first thing to disappear. Walk into any bakery on the island and you'll see them lined up next to the mallorcas and ensaimadas: small triangles of flaky puff pastry wrapped around a dollop of sweet cream cheese, baked until golden, and dusted with a sparkly coat of sugar. They're simple, they're fast, and they're dangerously addictive. You eat one and immediately reach for another. This recipe uses store-bought puff pastry — because even abuela would approve of that shortcut — and delivers a result that rivals anything from the panadería.

What are Quesitos?

Quesitos (literally "little cheeses") are small pastries made from puff pastry dough filled with a sweetened cream cheese mixture. They're folded into triangles, brushed with egg wash, sprinkled with sugar, and baked until they puff up into golden, flaky pockets. The name is a bit of a misnomer — they contain cream cheese, not queso, but the sweet cheese filling is the star of the show.

You'll find quesitos in every Puerto Rican bakery, usually sitting in a glass case next to the other pastries. They're a breakfast item, an afternoon snack, and a dessert — basically, there's no wrong time to eat one. The combination of shatteringly flaky pastry and smooth, sweet cream cheese filling is one of those perfect pairings that doesn't need improving.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
12 servings

Makes 12 pastries:

  • 1 puff pastry (2 sheets) — thawed but still cold. Pepperidge Farm or any all-butter brand works.
  • 8 cream cheese, softened — full-fat for the best flavor and texture.
  • 1/3 sugar — sweetens the filling without making it cloying.
  • 1 vanilla extract — rounds out the cream cheese flavor.
  • 1 egg, beaten — for the egg wash that gives them their golden shine.
  • Sugar for sprinkling — coarse sugar looks prettiest, but regular granulated works fine.

Mainland Swaps

  • No puff pastry? Store-bought is standard — even the best bakeries in Puerto Rico use it. Pepperidge Farm is available at every grocery store. Don't try to make puff pastry from scratch unless you have a free afternoon and a rolling pin.
  • Add guava? Absolutely. Add a small spoonful of guava paste on top of the cream cheese before folding for a classic Puerto Rican flavor combo.
  • Low-fat cream cheese? It works, but the filling won't be as rich or creamy. Full-fat is worth it here.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Preheat and prep. Preheat oven to 400°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper. Let the puff pastry thaw according to package directions — it should be pliable but still cold.
2
Make the filling. In a bowl, beat the softened cream cheese with sugar and vanilla until smooth and creamy. No lumps — take the time to get this right.
3
Cut the pastry. Unfold each puff pastry sheet and cut into 6 equal squares — that gives you 12 total. A pizza cutter makes this quick and clean.
4
Fill and fold. Place about a tablespoon of cream cheese filling in the center of each square. Fold each square diagonally into a triangle. Press the edges with a fork to seal — this also gives them that classic crimped look.
5
Egg wash and sugar. Brush each quesito generously with beaten egg. Sprinkle with sugar — don't be shy, that sugar crust is part of the magic.
6
Bake 12-15 minutes. Bake until puffed and deep golden brown. The pastry should be crispy and the filling just set. Let cool for 5 minutes before eating — if you can wait that long.

Tips for Perfect Quesitos

  • Keep the pastry cold. Warm puff pastry is a nightmare to work with and won't puff properly. If it gets sticky, pop it back in the fridge for 10 minutes.
  • Seal well. Use a fork to crimp the edges firmly. If the seams open during baking, the filling leaks out and you lose that creamy center.
  • Don't overfill. A generous tablespoon is enough. Too much filling bursts through the pastry and creates a mess on the baking sheet.
  • Bake until truly golden. Pale quesitos are underbaked and the pastry will be chewy instead of flaky. Wait for that deep golden color — it's worth the extra minute.

Store-bought is the way to go for quesitos. Puff pastry from scratch is a multi-hour project involving butter lamination and precise temperature control. The result is amazing, but even most Puerto Rican bakeries use commercially made dough. Pepperidge Farm or an all-butter puff pastry from the freezer section will give you excellent results.

Quesitos are best fresh, but they keep in an airtight container at room temperature for 2 days. To refresh them, warm in a 350°F oven for 3-4 minutes — this re-crisps the pastry. Refrigerating them makes the pastry soggy, so room temperature is better for short-term storage. You can also freeze unbaked quesitos on a tray, then transfer to a bag — bake from frozen, adding 2-3 extra minutes.
