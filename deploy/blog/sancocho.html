Sancocho

Puerto Rico's ultimate comfort stew — mixed meats and root vegetables simmered into a rich, hearty broth.

2 hours Medium

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·8 min read

Sancocho is Puerto Rico's ultimate comfort soup — a hearty stew packed with seven meats and root vegetables that's been simmering on the stove for hours. It's the dish that fixes everything: cold weather, heartbreak, homesickness, bad days. Every Puerto Rican family has their own version, passed down through generations with specific rules about which meats to use, how long to simmer, and whether the corn goes in whole or in pieces. The broth is the star — a deep, rich liquid that's absorbed the flavors of beef, pork, chicken, and every root vegetable you can find at the Latin grocery. When it's done right, a single bowl is a complete meal that warms you from the inside out. This recipe stays true to the traditional approach: mixed meats, real root vegetables, and enough broth to go around a table of ten.

What is Sancocho?

Sancocho is a thick, hearty stew that exists across Latin America and the Caribbean, but the Puerto Rican version is distinctive. It's built on a foundation of multiple meats — typically beef, pork, and chicken — simmered low and slow with a mix of tropical root vegetables called viandas. The broth develops over an hour or more, as the meats break down and release their collagen and flavor into the liquid, while the root vegetables absorb the savory goodness and thicken the stew naturally.

In Puerto Rico, sancocho is more than a recipe — it's a ritual. It's what families make after hurricanes when the power is out and the gas stove is the only heat source. It's what abuela makes when the whole family comes to visit. It's the dish that warms up a cold mountain evening in Utuado or Jayuya. The specific combination of root vegetables varies by region and family preference, but the essentials are always there: yuca for its starchy silkiness, plantain for body, and corn for sweetness. Some families add calabaza (West Indian pumpkin), some add green bananas, and some insist on specific cuts of meat. But the soul of the dish — meat, roots, sofrito, time — stays the same.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
10 servings

For 10 servings:

  • 2 lbs mixed meats (beef, pork, chicken) — a combination of bone-in pieces works best. Beef chuck, pork shoulder, and chicken thighs are ideal cuts.
  • 1 green plantain, peeled and cubed — green, not ripe. The starchy plantain thickens the broth and adds substance.
  • 2 yuca pieces — cassava root, peeled and cut into 2-inch chunks. Remove the fibrous center string.
  • 2 name pieces — white or yellow yam, peeled and cubed. Adds earthy sweetness to the broth.
  • 1 corn on cob, cut into pieces — each piece about 2 inches. The corn adds sweetness to the broth.
  • 1/4 cup sofrito — the aromatic base that ties everything together.
  • 1 packet sazon — sazon con culantro y achiote for color and depth.
  • 8 cups water — enough to cover everything with room for the vegetables.
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh cilantro for garnish — torn leaves and tender stems.

Mainland Swaps

  • No yuca? Yukon Gold potatoes are the closest substitute — they break down similarly and thicken the broth. Use the same amount.
  • No green plantain? Green banana works as a substitute with a slightly different texture. Ripe plantain will make the stew too sweet.
  • No name? Extra potato or parsnip can stand in. The flavor won't be exactly the same, but the starchiness serves the same purpose.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Brown the meats. Heat a large, heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add the mixed meats in batches and brown on all sides. Don't crowd the pot — you want a good sear, not steaming. Remove and set aside.
2
Build the broth. Return the meats to the pot and add 8 cups of water. Bring to a rolling boil, then skim any foam or impurities that rise to the surface. This gives you a cleaner-tasting broth.
3
Add the flavor base. Stir in the sofrito and sazon packet. This is where the broth starts to develop its characteristic color and aroma.
4
Add the root vegetables. Drop in the cubed plantain, yuca, name, and corn pieces. The pot should look like a harvest — colorful and overflowing. Bring back to a simmer.
5
Simmer low and slow. Reduce heat to medium-low and simmer uncovered for 1 to 1.5 hours. The meats should be fork-tender and the broth should be rich and thick from the starchy vegetables breaking down.
6
Season and serve. Taste the broth and adjust with salt and pepper — it will need it after all that water. Ladle into deep bowls and serve with white rice on the side. Top with fresh cilantro.

Tips for Perfect Sancocho

  • Brown the meats first. This step builds the flavor foundation. A good sear adds depth to the broth that you can't get from just boiling. Don't skip it.
  • Skim the foam. After you add the water and bring it to a boil, skim off the foam and fat that rises to the surface. This gives you a cleaner broth that looks and tastes better.
  • Don't rush the simmer. Sancocho needs time. The minimum is 1 hour, but 1.5 hours is better. The meats need time to become tender and the root vegetables need time to release their starch into the broth.
  • Season at the end. The broth reduces as it simmers, concentrating the salt. Always taste and adjust at the end rather than over-salting at the start.
