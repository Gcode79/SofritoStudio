Common Questions

What is sofrito?
Sofrito is the flavor base of Puerto Rican cooking — a blended mix of recao (culantro), garlic, onion, bell pepper, and herbs. Every dish starts with it.
Can I cook boricua food if I'm not Puerto Rican?
Absolutely. Every product is written for home cooks of all levels, with step-by-step instructions, ingredient swaps for mainland stores, and bilingual text.

I grew up in the Ortiz kitchen, learning from abuelas who measured with their hands and never wrote anything down. When I moved off the island, the recipes went with me — but only in memory. Every product here is bilingual, tested in the Ortiz kitchen, and refined until it just works. Whether you're feeding three kids on a Tuesday or roasting pernil for Nochebuena — you're not just making food, you're keeping the tradition alive.

Recaito is the herb-and-pepper base with no tomatoes, while sofrito on the island adds tomato or tomato sauce. On the mainland, "sofrito" is usually the tomato-free recaito-style green paste every Puerto Rican dish starts with.
Recao (culantro), ají dulce, and sofrito are sold frozen or jarred at most Latin grocery stores nationwide. Fresh recao is also sold as "sawtooth coriander" at Asian and Indian markets, and every recipe includes a mainland swap.
