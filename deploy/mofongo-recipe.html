Mofongo: The Complete Guide

Green plantains, garlic, chicharrón — the step-by-step to perfect mofongo every time, plus 5 variations.

35 minutes Serves 4

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·12 min read

What Is Mofongo?

Mofongo is Puerto Rico's most iconic dish — fried green plantains mashed with garlic, olive oil, and chicharrón (pork cracklings) into a dense, savory dome. It's comfort food, celebration food, and "I need something real right now" food.

The word comes from the Angolan Kimbundu word mfongo, reflecting the African roots of this dish. Enslaved Africans brought the technique of pounding starchy vegetables, and Puerto Ricans made it their own with garlic and pork. Every bite is history.

Ingredients

For 4 servings:

  • 4 green plantains — must be green, not yellow. Green = starchy and firm. Yellow = sweet and soft (that's for tostones or amarillos).
  • 6 cloves garlic — minced, not pressed. You want garlic pieces, not garlic juice.
  • 1/2 cup chicharrón — crushed. Buy pre-made at Latin stores, or make your own by frying pork skin until puffy.
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil — or butter for a richer flavor.
  • 1 teaspoon salt — adjust to taste.
  • 1/2 cup warm chicken broth — for moisture. Water works in a pinch.

Step-by-Step: How to Make Mofongo

1
Peel the plantains. Cut off both ends. Score the skin lengthwise (don't cut into the flesh). Pry the skin off with your thumbs — it should come off in 3 strips. This is the hardest part. Once you've done it twice, it's easy.
2
Cut into rounds. Slice each plantain into 1-inch thick rounds. Thicker = fluffier inside. Thinner = crispier outside.
3
Fry them. Heat oil to 350°F (175°C). Fry plantain rounds in batches until golden on the outside — about 3–4 minutes per side. They should be cooked through but not mushy. Drain on paper towels.
4
Mash while hot. Transfer hot plantains to a pilón (wooden mortar) or large bowl. Add garlic, chicharrón, olive oil, and salt. Mash vigorously. The heat softens the plantains — you have about 2 minutes before they cool and get stiff.
5
Add broth gradually. Pour warm broth a little at a time, mashing between additions. Stop when the texture is dense but holds together — not dry, not mushy. Think "thick mashed potatoes with personality."
6
Shape and serve. Pack into a small bowl, flip onto the plate to form a dome. Make a well in the top for gravy, shrimp, or whatever protein you're serving. Serve immediately — mofongo waits for no one.

5 Mofongo Variations

Clásico

Garlic + chicharrón + olive oil. The original. Nothing else needed.

Mofongo con Camarones

Shrimp in garlic butter sauce, spooned into the well. The most popular restaurant version.

Mofongo Relleno

Stuffed with shredded beef (ropa vieja) or chicken in creole sauce.

Mofongo de Yuca

Swap plantains for yuca (cassava). Chewier, denser, equally delicious.

Mofongo con Pernil

Slow-roasted pulled pork on top. Sunday dinner energy.

Pro Tips

  • Use a pilón. A wooden mortar and pestle gives the best texture — slightly chunky with visible garlic pieces. A potato masher works but is less authentic.
  • Work fast. Once plantains hit the mortar, you have 2 minutes before they cool. Have everything prepped.
  • Green plantains only. Yellow plantains are sweet — that's for amarillos (sweet plantains), not mofongo.
  • Toast your garlic. For deeper flavor, fry the minced garlic in olive oil for 30 seconds before adding to the mortar.
  • Make it ahead. Shape mofongo into domes, refrigerate, and re-fry at 350°F for 3 minutes when ready to serve. Nearly as good as fresh.
