How to Make Puerto Rican Sofrito

The base of every Puerto Rican dish — real steps, bilingual captions, no culinary degree required.

What you'll learn

Sofrito is the flavor foundation of Puerto Rican cooking — recao, garlic, onion, and bell pepper, blended into a paste that powers arroz con gandules, beans, pollo guisado, and just about everything else.

  • The four ingredients that make real sofrito.
  • How to blend it into a smooth flavor base.
  • The caldero method — toast your sofrito until the garlic turns golden.
  • Coat every grain of rice, add broth, cover, and simmer.

Transcript

  1. Welcome to Sofrito Studio — Boricua cooking made simple.
  2. Today we're making sofrito, the base of every Puerto Rican dish.
  3. Start with recao, garlic, onion, and bell pepper.
  4. Blend until smooth — that's your flavor foundation.
  5. Heat olive oil in your caldero and add two spoonfuls.
  6. Listen to that sizzle — that's flavor building.
  7. Stir for two minutes until the garlic turns golden.
  8. Now add your rice — one cup, rinsed and ready.
  9. Stir to coat every grain in that sofrito goodness.
  10. Add broth, cover, and simmer — dinner in 30 minutes.
