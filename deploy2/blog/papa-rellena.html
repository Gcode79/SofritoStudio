Papa Rellena

Stuffed potato balls with seasoned beef filling — crispy, golden, and worth every calorie.

1 hour Medium

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·6 min read

Papa rellena are stuffed potato balls — the fried comfort food that's worth every calorie. Smooth mashed potato wrapped around a pocket of seasoned ground beef, breaded, and deep-fried until the outside is shatteringly crispy while the inside stays creamy and hot. Every Puerto Rican cook has their own version, and debates about whose are best are legendary at family gatherings. Some use more olives, some add raisins, some insist on a specific ratio of potato to filling. But the core idea is always the same: take two humble ingredients — potatoes and ground beef — and transform them into something that makes people close their eyes and moan with the first bite. This recipe makes 8 generous papa rellenas, which is never enough.

What is Papa Rellena?

Papa rellena — literally "stuffed potato" — is one of Puerto Rico's most iconic frituras. It starts with smooth, creamy mashed potatoes formed into a ball around a savory ground beef filling called picadillo, then breaded and deep-fried until golden. The contrast between the crispy exterior and the soft, steamy interior is what makes this dish irresistible. The beef filling is typically seasoned with sofrito, sazon, tomato sauce, and olives — the same base that flavors so many Puerto Rican dishes.

You'll find papa rellena at kiosks, bakeries, and family gatherings across Puerto Rico. They're a popular party food because they can be assembled ahead and fried just before serving. In the diaspora, they're the dish that every boricua makes when they want to impress guests or satisfy a craving for the real thing. Some families serve them with a drizzle of ketchup, others with a garlic mayo, and purists eat them plain to appreciate the contrast of textures. Whatever way you serve them, papa rellena is proof that the simplest ingredients, treated with care, can produce something extraordinary.

Ingredients

Scale recipe:
8 servings

Makes 8 pieces:

  • 2 potatoes, peeled — starchy potatoes like Russets work best. They mash smoothly and hold together when formed into balls.
  • 1 ground beef — regular 80/20 for flavor. Extra lean will make the filling dry.
  • 1/4 sofrito — the aromatic base for the beef filling.
  • 1 sazon — sazon con culantro y achiote for color and depth in the filling.
  • 1/2 tomato sauce — gives the beef filling moisture and tomato richness.
  • 1/4 olives, chopped — manzanilla olives with pimento. The briny pops in the filling are essential.
  • 1 egg, beaten — for the egg wash that helps the flour coating stick.
  • 1/2 flour — all-purpose flour for dredging before the egg wash.
  • Oil for frying — vegetable or canola oil with a high smoke point.

Mainland Swaps

  • No sofrito? Mince 3 garlic cloves + 1/4 cup diced onion + 1/4 cup diced green pepper. Sauté until soft before adding the beef.
  • No sazon? Use 1 tsp paprika + 1/2 tsp garlic powder + 1/4 tsp cumin. The color won't be as orange, but the flavor works.
  • Want to bake instead of fry? You can bake at 400F for 25 minutes, but you'll lose the crispy exterior that makes papa rellena special.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Boil and mash the potatoes. Place peeled potatoes in a large pot, cover with salted water, and boil until fork-tender (about 15 minutes). Drain completely and mash until smooth — no lumps. Let cool slightly.
2
Make the picadillo filling. Brown the ground beef in a skillet over medium-high heat, breaking it up as it cooks. Add the sofrito, sazon, tomato sauce, and chopped olives. Cook for 5 minutes until the flavors meld. Remove from heat and let cool completely — warm filling will melt the potato.
3
Form the balls. Take about 1/3 cup of mashed potato and flatten it in your palm. Add a generous spoonful of the cooled beef filling in the center. Fold the potato over and seal into a smooth ball, about the size of a tennis ball. Repeat with remaining potato and filling.
4
Bread the balls. Dredge each potato ball in flour, shaking off the excess. Then dip in the beaten egg, letting the excess drip off. This two-step coating creates the crispy crust when fried.
5
Fry until golden. Heat oil to 350F in a deep skillet or pot. Carefully lower the potato balls into the oil and fry for 3-4 minutes, turning occasionally, until deep golden brown on all sides. Drain on paper towels.
6
Serve hot. Let them cool for a minute or two — the filling is molten hot. Serve with ketchup, garlic mayo, or just eat them plain. They're best the moment they come out of the oil.

Tips for Perfect Papa Rellena

  • Cool the filling completely. This is the most important tip. If the beef filling is even slightly warm when you form the balls, the potato will soften and the ball will fall apart in the oil. Refrigerate the filling for 20 minutes if needed.
  • Mash the potatoes thoroughly. Any lumps in the potato will create weak spots where the ball can crack during frying. Mash them until completely smooth, or pass them through a ricer.
  • Don't make them too big. Tennis-ball sized is the sweet spot. Larger balls are harder to fry evenly and may burst in the oil. Smaller ones overcook before the filling heats through.
  • Maintain oil temperature. If the oil drops below 350F, the breading absorbs too much oil and turns greasy. Fry in small batches to keep the temperature steady.
