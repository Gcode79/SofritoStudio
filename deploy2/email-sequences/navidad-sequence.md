## Email 2 — Oct 15

**Subject:** The pernil timeline (start planning now)
**Preview text:** Perfect pernil takes planning — here's exactly when to do what.

Let's talk about pernil.

The Puerto Rican roast pork shoulder is the centerpiece of Nochebuena. Every boricua family has their version, their secrets, their opinions about the right way to do it. But one thing everyone agrees on: you can't rush it.

Here's the timeline:

**5-7 days before Nochebuena:** Make your adobo mojado (wet marinade). Get the garlic, oregano, olive oil, vinegar, and sazón ready.

**2-3 days before:** Marinate the pernil. The longer it sits, the deeper the flavor penetrates. Some people do 24 hours. My abuela did 3 days.

**Day of (Dec 24):** Start cooking early. A 10-pound pernil takes 4-5 hours at 325°F. You want it done by 6 PM? Start at noon.

**The last 30 minutes:** Crank the heat to 425°F to get that skin — the cuero — crispy and golden. This is non-negotiable.

## Email 4 — Nov 15

**Subject:** Pernil secrets from my abuela
**Preview text:** The tips that make pernil taste like abuela's — every time.

My abuela didn't write down her pernil recipe. She didn't need to — she'd made it so many times it was muscle memory. But she did share a few secrets before she passed, and I've carried them ever since.

**Secret 1: The garlic goes deep.**
Don't just rub the outside. Cut slits in the meat and stuff whole garlic cloves inside. As the pork roasts, the garlic melts into the meat and infuses it from the inside out.

**Secret 2: Low and slow, then hot.**
Cook at 325°F for most of the time. The meat should be tender enough to pull apart with a fork. Then — and only then — crank it to 425°F for the last 20-30 minutes to crisp the cuero. The skin should crack when you tap it.

**Secret 3: The drippings are gold.**
Collect every drop of liquid that comes off that pernil. Strain it. Use it as the base for your rice. Use it to moisten the meat. Never waste it.

**Secret 4: Let it rest.**
When it comes out of the oven, tent it with foil and let it rest for at least 30 minutes. The juices redistribute. The meat stays moist. This is the hardest part because the smell will make you want to carve immediately. Don't.

These are the kinds of details that make the difference between "good pernil" and "pernil that makes people cry."
