## Email 3 — Day 4

**Subject:** The 5 ingredients every boricua kitchen needs
**Preview text:** Stock your pantry with these essentials — here's where to find them.

You can't cook Puerto Rican food without these five staples in your kitchen:

**1. Sofrito**
The base of everything. Peppers, onions, garlic, cilantro, recao — blended and frozen in cubes. If you're not making your own, Goya and Loisa both sell good versions in the freezer aisle of most Latin grocery stores.

**2. Sazón**
That golden color and earthy flavor? That's sazón. Sazón Goya with coriander and annatto is the classic. Buy the packets — they keep forever.

**3. Adobo**
All-purpose seasoning. Every boricua has a container of Goya Adobo in their cabinet. Use it on chicken, pork, rice, beans — everything.

**4. Manzanilla Olives (with pimentos)**
These go in everything from arroz con pollo to stewed beans. The briny, salty bite is non-negotiable.

**5. Recao (Culantro)**
Not the same as cilantro — recao has a stronger, more earthy flavor. If you can't find it fresh, frozen recao leaves work. Some Latin grocery stores carry frozen recao puree too.

**Can't find these locally?** Most Latin grocery stores carry all five. If you don't have one nearby, Amazon and Walmart.com stock Goya's full line. I also wrote a full guide on where to buy Puerto Rican ingredients online.

## Email 4 — Day 6

**Subject:** The recipe that made my abuela proud
**Preview text:** Arroz con gandules — the recipe every boricua family argues about.

Every Puerto Rican family has an opinion about whose arroz con gandules is the best. My abuela's was simple: no shortcuts, no extra ingredients, just rice, gandules, sofrito, and patience.

Here's the version I grew up with:

**Arroz con Gandules**

Ingredients:
- 2 cups long-grain rice
- 1 can gandules (pigeon peas), drained
- 3 tbsp sofrito
- 1 packet sazón con azafrán
- 2 tbsp tomato sauce
- 1½ cups water or chicken broth
- 2 tbsp oil
- Salt to taste

Method:
Heat oil in a caldero over medium heat. Add sofrito and cook 2 minutes until fragrant. Add tomato sauce and sazón, stir. Add gandules and cook 2 minutes. Add water, bring to a boil. Add rice, stir once, then cover and reduce heat to low. Cook 25 minutes without lifting the lid. Fluff with a fork.

That's it. The secret isn't technique — it's not peeking. My abuela would swat my hand if I lifted that lid.
