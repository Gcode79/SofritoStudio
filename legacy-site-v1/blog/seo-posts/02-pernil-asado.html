Pernil Asado — Puerto Rican Roasted Pork Shoulder Recipe

Slow-roasted pork shoulder with crispy cuero (skin), seasoned with sofrito, garlic, and oregano. The Ortiz family's Nochebuena classic.

⏱ 5.5 hours 🍽 12 servings

By Josh Ortiz·August 2026·10 min read

Jump to Recipe

You haven't experienced a Puerto Rican Nochebuena until you've smelled pernil roasting in the oven. That aroma — garlic, oregano, slow-rendered pork fat filling every room of the house — is the smell of Christmas in a boricua home. It's the smell of abuela's kitchen at 6 AM, the pork already in the oven, the sofrito doing its work while the rest of the family sleeps.

Pernil asado — slow-roasted pork shoulder — is the centerpiece of every Puerto Rican holiday table. It's not a quick recipe. It takes 5 to 6 hours of low, slow roasting. But the result is pork so tender it falls apart with a fork, topped with a layer of cuero — crispy, crackling pork skin that shatters like glass and tastes like pure joy. If you've never had pernil cuero, you haven't lived.

My abuela's pernil was legendary. She'd start seasoning it the night before, pushing her garlic-and-oregano mojo deep into every crevice of the meat. By the time the family arrived on Christmas Eve, the pernil had been roasting for hours, and the whole block knew the Ortiz house was open for business. Neighbors would drift over, drawn by the smell. Nobody was ever turned away.

This Puerto Rican pernil recipe is the one she taught me — tested and refined until it works in any oven, anywhere. I've included every trick for crispy cuero, every substitution for mainland cooks, and every tip for making this the best pernil you've ever had. Whether it's Nochebuena or just a Sunday you want to feel special, this pernil delivers.

What You'll Need (Kitchen Tools)

  • Large Roasting Pan with Rack — Holds an 8-10 lb shoulder. A rack keeps the pork elevated so the cuero crisps evenly.
  • Instant-Read Meat Thermometer — No guessing. 195°F means tender, pull-apart perfection.
  • Sharp Chef's Knife — For scoring the cuero. A dull knife won't cut cleanly through the skin.
  • Pilón (Mortar & Pestle) — Pound the garlic and oregano into a paste the old-school way. The texture is different from a food processor.

Pernil Asado

Pernil Asado — Lechón del horno, piel crujiente

Prep: 30 min Cook: 5 hours Total: 5.5 hours Servings: 12

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in pork shoulder (8-10 lbs)
  • 4 tablespoons sofrito
  • 10 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 tablespoons dried oregano
  • 1 tablespoon paprika
  • 1 packet sazón con culantro y achiote
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
  • 1 cup water (for roasting pan)

Instructions

  1. Score the cuero. Using a sharp knife, cut a crosshatch pattern through the skin, about 1 inch apart. Cut through the skin but not deep into the meat.
  2. Make the mojo. Combine sofrito, garlic, olive oil, oregano, paprika, sazón, salt, pepper, and vinegar in a bowl.
  3. Season everything. Rub the mojo over the entire shoulder. Push it into the scores. Get it under the skin where possible. Be generous — this is not the time for restraint.
  4. Marinate overnight. Place the pork skin-side up on a rack in the roasting pan. Refrigerate uncovered for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. This dries the cuero for maximum crispiness.
  5. Roast low and slow. Preheat oven to 350°F. Add 1 cup water to the pan bottom. Roast 4-5 hours, basting every 45 minutes with pan juices. Target internal temp: 195°F.
  6. Crisp the cuero. Increase oven to 425°F for the last 30 minutes. Watch carefully — the skin should puff, crackle, and turn golden brown.
  7. Rest and carve. Rest 20 minutes before carving. Spoon pan juices over the sliced meat. Serve the cuero pieces on the side.
👨‍🍳 Chef's Notes:
  • The cuero is everything. Refrigerating the pork uncovered overnight dries the skin, which is the secret to crackling. Wet skin = chewy skin.
  • Baste, don't flip. Always baste with the skin side up. Flipping steams the cuero and ruins the crisp.
  • 195°F is the magic number. At this temperature, the collagen has fully rendered and the meat is fall-apart tender.
  • Save the pan drippings. Mix with water or broth for an incredible gravy. Puerto Ricans call this "salsa de pernil."
Nutrition (per serving): 420 cal · 38g protein · 3g carbs · 28g fat · 0g fiber · 520mg sodium

Mainland US Substitutions

  • Pork shoulder: Any bone-in pork shoulder (also called pork butt or Boston butt) from any US grocery store works. 8-10 lbs feeds 10-12 people. Ask your butcher to score the cuero if you're not confident with a knife.
  • Sofrito: Goya brand jarred sofrito in a pinch. Homemade is better. Make our recipe.
  • Sazón: Goya sazón is at every major grocery store. See our arroz con gandules post for DIY substitutes.
  • Oregano: Mediterranean oregano works. Puerto Rican oregano (oregano brujo) is stronger — use 1.5x if you find it at a Latin market.
👵 Abuela's Tip: "El cuero se seca en la nevera toda la noche." — The skin must dry overnight in the fridge. Put the seasoned pork on a rack, uncovered, and let the cold air do its work. By morning, the skin will be tacky and dry — exactly what you want for crackling. Don't skip this step."
