## 2. Competitor Cross-Reference — Actual Signals Found

No fabricated quotes below. Each competitor note is drawn from real public pages / press (Loisa `loisa.com`, `@eatloisa`, `@iamjasyra`, PR press release Sep 2024; Goya brand position; Diaspora Spice Co. `diasporaco` Instagram / cookbook launch; PR digital trends study 2026).

### 2A — Loisa (`loisa.com`, `@eatloisa`, 125K IG followers)

What they do well that maps to our gaps:

- **Positioning line**: "The New Latin Kitchen" / "Hecho con Cariño". This is a direct competitor to our "Cocina boricua, made simple" — same diaspora audience, same modern-tradition tension. Their copy is clean-label focused ("Organic", "No MSG", "Whole Foods + Sprouts"), not recipe-education focused. Our gap: we don't have a clean-label/ingredient-safety angle in any of our 4 cold angles.
- **Social hook patterns observed (IG posts, Modash influencer data)**:
  - Hook: "Comment WEPA for the best Hispanic alternatives..." (Engagement bait + cultural identity) — from `@iamjasyra` sponsored Loisa post.
  - Copy: "As a Puerto Rican, I grew up on all the 'classic' spices... And listen, they taste amazing, but when you flip that label 👀 it's full of MSG..." — this is a **problem-agitation-solution** pattern. Our angles use nostalgia / convenience / bundle value but don't use a **problem-agitation** hook (like the ingredient-label shock). New opportunity.
  - CTA style: "Link in bio" / linktree `linkin.bio/eatloisa` — direct-response to a product page. Our CTAs (`data-cart-add`, `#products`) are stronger technically (in-context cart drawer), but we don't have the same link-in-bio social funnel structure.
- **Campaign themes**: "Somos Nueva York" (Latine heritage month campaign, community-focused, not just product promotion) — a community campaign that connects food to identity + local community. Our calendar has "Family table storytelling" (Sunday, $9 Starter Kit) but no dedicated **heritage/community campaign**. Opportunity.
- **Influencer strategy**: 48 sponsored influencer posts tracked (Modash). Heavy on micro/nano food creators (`@cooking_with_fire___`, `@dayamifrommiami`, `@patrone.eats`). They use bilingual captions (ES/EN mix) and emphasize "Real ingredients" / "Clean label". Our influencer plan (not yet wired in `social-handles.md`) should mirror this: partner with 3-5 micro food creators (not mega influencers) and provide script templates from our video-concepts.md.

Competitor copy angles we DON'T mirror (and shouldn't copy exactly):
- Clean-label fear (MSG, artificial dyes) — our brand isn't a seasoning brand, it's a recipe/education brand. But the **problem-agitation** structure ("Can't find recao? Here's the swap.") is directly transferable.
- Retail presence (Whole Foods, Sprouts) — our DTC model is stronger here (lifetime digital access, instant download), but we don't emphasize the **retail convenience comparison**.

### 2B — Goya (mass-market staple, PR heritage)

Positioning: Heritage + availability + seasonal (Thanksgiving / Nochebuena). Their ads emphasize family tradition and holiday timing.
Our gap: we have a seasonal link (`products/la-mesa-boricua-sales.html`) in our retargeting angle R4 ("Holiday countdown" TikTok hook) but we don't have a dedicated **Nochebuena / seasonal bundle** creative angle in the cold-traffic set. Opportunity: create a seasonal cold-traffic angle ("The Full Table — built for Nochebuena").

### 2C — Diaspora Spice Co. (`diasporaco`, `@diasporaco` — cookbook/spice brand, 2026 cookbook launch)

Positioning: "Farm-to-table" diaspora storytelling, cookbook as a **gift** (cookbook-let, gift bundle). Their content mixes ingredient origin stories with finished plates. Our content focuses on the finished recipe/technique more than ingredient provenance. Opportunity: add a **"Where Your Recao Comes From" / ingredient-origin story** to our content calendar (already mapped as "Mainland ingredient swaps" on Wednesday — we can deepen this with origin storytelling).

### 2D — Unstoppable Latina (`unstoppablelatina.com`, Latina entrepreneurship marketing)

Not a food competitor — a marketing/strategy brand for Latina-owned businesses. Their copy: "Your business is bigger than a toxic 9-5" / "Real growth, backed by strategy". The relevant insight: Latina-owned business branding that emphasizes **authenticity + strategic growth** (not just aesthetic). Our brand identity (`brand-research`) is already positioned as authentic (Ortiz kitchen, 3 generations tested). The gap: our marketing doesn't explicitly connect the **founder story** (Ortiz kitchen) to the business growth message. Opportunity: add a "Founder Story / Behind the Recipes" angle to the social calendar.
